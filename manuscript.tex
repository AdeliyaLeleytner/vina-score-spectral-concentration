\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background.}
Dense docking matrices conflate spectral redundancy, target--target correlation maps and
compound-level ranking.

\textbf{Results.}
Two Vina matrices (12,651 ligands by 44 targets and 260,060 by 58) were dominated by a nearly
uniform ligand-wide axis; within-ligand centring exposed residual maps with PR effective
dimensions \DffResidualPR{} and \DsResidualPR{} for correlation spectra and
\DffResidualCovariancePR{} and \DsResidualCovariancePR{} for covariance spectra; only the
increase direction was robust across estimands.
The maps were library conditional: in post-hoc
within-library splits, low-
versus high-molecular-weight maps agreed at only \DffMWDomainRho{} and
\DsMWDomainRho{}, versus \DffMWDomainControl{} and \DsMWDomainControl{} for
molecular-weight-matched chemically disjoint controls. In support-specific sampling, 200
same-distribution ligands recovered source-library maps at \DffProbeTwoHundred{} and
\DsProbeTwoHundred{}. Physicochemical and count-Morgan bases predicted similar score
structure out of chemical group, whereas a stable-hash component with mean held-out target
$R^2$ near zero still resembled experimental correlation maps because column correlation
removed its scale. Against a fixed centred endpoint from four published kinase panels,
docking-side centring increments of 0.001--0.045 were unresolved; against uncentred endpoints
they were 0.168--0.264. Subtracting a ligand's row mean is exactly invariant to its target
order, while unequal target scaling converts that term into a rank-one correction. On
\BroadRankingEvaluatedLigands{} ligands and \BroadRankingPairs{} non-tied observed
within-ligand target comparisons, absolute Vina scored \BroadRankingAbsolute{}
versus \BroadRankingResidual{} for scaled residuals; an endpoint-restricted human binding
Ki/Kd arm gave
\BroadHumanKiKdAbsolute{} versus \BroadHumanKiKdResidual{}, with chemical and target-
composition intervals spanning zero. Its \BroadRankingAbsoluteMinusDockingPrior{} advantage
over a target-only prior fitted on the external DOCKSTRING reference was likewise unresolved.

\textbf{Conclusions.}
In the tested Vina systems, a target-correlation map is an observable of the scoring
pipeline, receptor panel and observed source library: inexpensive to estimate on a
support-specific representative sample, poorly portable across the tested molecular-size
domains, and not a compound-level ranking rule.
Residual dimension is not a stand-alone docking-quality criterion.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Scientific Contribution.}
We quantify molecular-size-domain non-transport and support-specific recovery of docking target-
correlation maps, and identify a scale-free nuisance-projection failure in validating fitted
component maps. We also make explicit that within-ligand centring cannot alter target order and
identify unequal target scaling as the operation that breaks this invariance.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} molecular docking; AutoDock Vina; scoring-function bias;
target-correlation structure; effective dimension; score normalization; kinase selectivity;
reverse docking.

\section{Introduction}

A docking calculation produces one score for one ligand--target pose, but many
cheminformatics applications use a matrix. Reverse docking compares targets for a compound,
secondary-pharmacology panels search for off-target liabilities, and multiobjective design
optimizes activity profiles rather than a single interaction. In these settings the object
of interest is a matrix $X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times P}$, with ligands in rows and targets in
columns. Treating its $P$ columns as $P$ independent descriptors is unwarranted when a
small number of shared score tendencies dominates the matrix.

Those three applications turn on a compound's target \emph{order}, and we show below that
the most common correction cannot change it. The object that a docking panel does support is
the target-pair map: which score columns co-vary across a stated ligand library. That map has
real uses---diagnosing how redundant a screening panel is, choosing which targets a new panel
should contain, and comparing two scoring set-ups on a shared ligand support---and this
article is about how far it can be trusted, and about the sharp line between it and
compound-level ranking.

Keeping those two apart requires being explicit about what is being estimated. We use
\emph{estimand} in the sense of ICH E9(R1) \cite{ich2019estimand}: the precise specification
of the quantity under estimation---here, jointly, which score transformation, which
experimental endpoint, which ligand support and which target panel. Two analyses that differ
in any one of these components estimate different things even when they report the same
summary statistic, and Table~\ref{tab:glossary} fixes the terms this article uses.
The practical consequence recurs throughout: an apparent benefit of normalizing docking
scores can be produced entirely by transforming the experimental endpoint at the same time.

Two sources of shared variation have long been recognized. Empirical scoring functions can
reward additional ligand atoms and contacts, so molecular size and flexibility affect scores
across many targets \cite{pan2003,jacobsson2006,carta2007}. Protein pockets also differ in size, contact area and
composition, producing target-specific score offsets. Such effects motivated several
ligand-wise, target-wise and bidirectional score normalizations for virtual screening and
target fishing \cite{vigers2004,jacobsson2006,sepresa2009,yang2010cpi,luo2017,lapillo2019,lee2020crds}.
Following the two-factor chemical--protein-interactome model of Yang et al., we write
\begin{equation}
X_{ij}=\mu+a_i+b_j+\gamma_{ij},
\label{eq:additive}
\end{equation}
where $a_i$ is a ligand-wide favourable-score tendency, $b_j$ is a target offset, and
$\gamma_{ij}$ is the remaining ligand--target term. The spectrum of the full matrix may be
dominated by $a_i$ even when $\gamma_{ij}$ contains several correlated directions.

Matrix-wide correction and multivariate analysis of docking panels predate this work. MASC
standardized each ligand's scores across multiple active sites, while later studies showed
that its enrichment effect depends on the reference target panel and scoring function.
Fukunishi et al. derived receptor- and compound-similarity from a multi-receptor by
multi-ligand docking-affinity matrix and subsequently applied PCA to a protein--compound
interaction matrix. SePreSA and DRAR-CPI used sequential drug- and protein-direction Z
transformations, while combined ligand- and target-score normalization was also used
operationally \cite{vigers2004,fukunishi2005,fukunishi2006mts,fukunishi2006,sepresa2009,
yang2010cpi,drarcpi2011,jacobsson2006,casey2009,fukunishi2018}.

These procedures are related to, but not algebraically identical with, the primary estimand
used here. MASC additionally divides every ligand row by its own standard deviation, whereas
2DIZ sequentially standardizes in both matrix directions; our residual target-pair geometry
subtracts ligand means and subsequently gives target columns unit variance. For any ligand
with positive row standard deviation, MASC row standardization is a positive affine
transformation across its targets and therefore preserves that ligand's target order. Because
the shift and scale are ligand-specific, it can nevertheless change the ordering of ligands
within any one target. We therefore
claim neither normalization nor docking-profile PCA as new. The contribution is to quantify
how target-correlation maps change with chemical support, establish how many representative
ligands recover a source-library map, characterize a low-dimensional ligand-feature response
within the residual, and distinguish map geometry from ligand-wise ranking.

Related work reinforces this distinction from two directions. Random-matrix methods have
used spectral structure to regularize chemical-feature correlations for affinity prediction,
whereas kinase-selectivity metrics can reorder compounds when the operational definition is
changed \cite{lee2016,graczyk2007,bosc2017}. Here PR is therefore used as a transparent dependence
scale, not introduced as a new statistic; the methodological contribution is the complete
audit that keeps matrix redundancy, target-pair co-response and within-ligand ranking
separate.

The same distinction applies to experimental validation. Let $G(X)$ denote the
target-by-target correlation matrix calculated across ligands. Comparing $G(X)$ with an
experimental matrix $G(E)$ asks whether pairs of targets co-vary similarly across two
libraries. Comparing a centred docking geometry $G_R(X)$ with a centred experimental
geometry $G_R(E)$ changes both predictor and endpoint. A difference between those two
matched comparisons cannot be assigned uniquely to transforming the docking scores. It is
also not a test of whether the more active target is ranked correctly for an individual
compound.

Two public, dense Vina-family matrices make it possible to quantify these distinctions at
scale. DOCKSTRING contains scores for more than 260,000 molecules against 58 medically
relevant targets \cite{dockstring2022}. A separately generated secondary-pharmacology matrix contains 12,651
analyzable compounds and 44 targets \cite{nikitin2025}. Four published kinase profiling resources---DAVIS, PKIS2, PKIS1 and
KiRHub---provide dense experimental measurements on a common set of 20 kinases
\cite{davis2011,drewry2017,elkins2016,saifudeen2026}.

The original DOCKSTRING report documented correlations among its target score columns
\cite{dockstring2022}. Our analysis starts from that observation but asks different questions:
whether a complete target-correlation map transports across ligand supports, how many
same-support ligands recover it, whether fitted-component maps survive nuisance controls,
and whether any matrix transformation improves ligand-wise target ordering.

We use these resources to separate four questions. First, what dependence remains after
removal of ligand main effects? Second, does that target-correlation map transport between
chemical domains, and how many representative ligands are needed to estimate it? Third,
what low-dimensional ligand-feature response organizes the residual map, and what can
agreement with fixed experimental co-response endpoints license? Fourth, does the same
processing improve ligand-wise target order?

The principal empirical finding answers the second question: in exploratory post hoc splits
within the two observed source libraries, low- and high-molecular-weight maps disagree sharply
despite near-ceiling agreement in molecular-weight-matched disjoint controls. Descriptive
same-distribution experiments show high recovery of each source-library map from 200--500
ligands; they do not define a universal sample-size or accuracy threshold.
The exact operational finding answers the fourth: subtracting a ligand's row mean cannot
change its target order, and only unequal target scaling turns that otherwise invariant term
into a rank-one ranking correction. The spectral and feature decompositions explain these
boundaries; they are not introduced as new PCA, centring or effective-rank estimators.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Study design and analysis status}

The study separates three estimands: the target-correlation spectrum of a dense score
matrix, the upper-triangular target-pair geometry across a stated ligand support, and
within-ligand observed-pair target concordance. Analyses of the two large score matrices and
the matched ChEMBL blocks were developed first. The fixed-20 experimental geometry,
structural controls, centering-panel comparison and sampling-recovery analyses are
exploratory post hoc analyses added during manuscript revision. The dense DAVIS and PKIS2
exact-match ranking checks, descriptor decomposition and molecular-size-domain splits were also
added during revision and were not preregistered. Molecular weight was selected as the
domain stratifier after preliminary inspection.
KiRHub was inspected only
after the 15-pair experimental endpoint had been fixed from DAVIS, PKIS2 and PKIS1, but this
sequence was not preregistered. Statistical probabilities are therefore interpreted as
calibrated descriptive evidence rather than confirmatory error control over the entire
analysis history.

\subsection{Large Vina score matrices}

Docking-44 was derived from the secondary-pharmacology resource of Nikitin et al.
\cite{nikitin2025}. It contains 12,651 analyzable compounds and 44 receptor columns scored
with Vina-GPU 2.0 \cite{ding2023}. The upstream workflow replaced positive scores with zero before release.
Accordingly, the frozen analyzable export contained no value above zero
(\DffFrozenZeroCells{} cells were exactly zero), and reapplying the upper-zero rule made
no further change. Its 8,159 missing cells
(1.466\%) were filled with the mean of their target column for the primary complete-matrix
analysis. Missingness-aware and complete-case alternatives were analyzed separately.

DOCKSTRING v1 contains \DsReleaseRows{} molecule rows and 58 target-score columns
\cite{dockstring2022}. The source table had \DsMissingCells{} missing scores
(\DsMissingPercent{}) across \DsIncompleteRows{} rows. Because listwise deletion removed
only \DsIncompleteRowPercent{} of molecules, DOCKSTRING-58 used the \DsAnalysisRows{}
rows complete across all targets, without imputation; target-mean and target-median
sensitivities retained every row. Scores were generated with the DOCKSTRING AutoDock Vina
workflow \cite{vina2010}. Of \DsReleasePositiveCells{} positive source scores,
\DsAnalysisPositiveCells{} occurred in retained rows and were set to zero in the primary
analysis; retaining them was a sensitivity. The local data manifest records the score matrix and derived molecular
identity contract; receptor files and target configurations remain available in the
checksum-anchored upstream DOCKSTRING release.

Cross-panel chemical overlap was calculated from recomputed Standard InChIKeys,
14-character connectivity blocks and canonical non-empty Bemis--Murcko scaffolds
\cite{murcko1996}. Target identities were curated by protein accession, and receptor files
were compared by structure identifier and chain. These counts characterize overlap; they
were not used to remove rows from the full spectral analyses.

\subsection{Experimental kinase panels}

All kinase-geometry analyses used the same ordered 20 targets: ABL1, AKT1, AKT2, CDK2,
CSF1R, EGFR, FGFR1, IGF1R, JAK2, KDR, KIT, LCK, MAP2K1, MAPK1, MAPK14, MAPKAPK2, MET,
PLK1, ROCK1 and SRC. DAVIS contributed a dense 72-by-20 pKd block from the CC0
DAVIS-Complete V3 export, which also supplies the compound structures and curated target
sequences used here \cite{wu2025davis}. The affinities originate from Davis et al.
\cite{davis2011}. The released 10,000-nM Kd upper bound becomes a pKd floor of 5:
\DavisFloorCells{} cells (\DavisFloorPercent{}) lie at that limit, with
\DavisFloorRange{} per target and \DavisHighFloorTargets{} targets above 90\%.
PKIS2 contributed 645 compounds measured by single-dose KINOMEscan
percentage displacement or inhibition at 1~$\mu$M \cite{drewry2017}. PKIS1 contributed 360 compounds measured by Nanosyn percentage inhibition
at 1~$\mu$M; duplicate compound identifiers were median-aggregated and the CDK2/cyclin A
construct was primary \cite{elkins2016}. KiRHub contributed a dense 92-by-20 residual-activity block measured at
1~$\mu$M at the ATP $K_m$; CDK2/cyclin A was primary \cite{saifudeen2026}. Correlation geometry is invariant to reversing the orientation of all
columns in a panel, so the pKd, inhibition and residual-activity directions do not create a
sign ambiguity in the target-pair comparisons.

The DAVIS censoring sensitivity replaced each pKd value by whether it lay above the pKd-5
floor, and separately retained only targets with at least 5, 10 or 15 values above that
floor. It also rebuilt the three-panel 15-pair endpoint after replacing continuous DAVIS
correlation by binary above-floor-status correlation, then evaluated the resulting labels
against centred KiRHub geometry. These analyses test robustness to the measurement limit;
they do not recover quantitative affinity information below it.

To prevent direct ligand reuse in the docking reference, Standard-InChI connectivity blocks
from DAVIS, PKIS2 and PKIS1 were unioned before extracting the 20-target DOCKSTRING matrix.
The 1,050 excluded blocks removed 481 DOCKSTRING rows, leaving 259,579 reference ligands.
An additional cyclic-Murcko exclusion and five fixed 15,000-ligand reference supports were
sensitivities. KiRHub source tables contain compound names but no structures, so KiRHub-
specific molecular-identity exclusion was not possible.

Overlap among the three structure-annotated experimental panels was audited at three
levels: full Standard InChIKey, its 14-character connectivity block and canonical non-empty
Bemis--Murcko scaffold. Empty scaffolds were excluded from cross-panel scaffold
intersections. Because KiRHub reports compound names without a frozen structure mapping,
only exact normalized-name overlap with DAVIS was evaluated; PKIS project identifiers were
not treated as comparable clinical names.

\subsection{Centering, correlation spectra and effective dimension}

For a complete matrix $X$, fitting the additive structure in Eq.~\ref{eq:additive} by least
squares gives the two-way-centred residual
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{ij}=X_{ij}-\bar X_{i\cdot}-\bar X_{\cdot j}+\bar X_{\cdot\cdot}.
\label{eq:twoway}
\end{equation}
The primary spectrum is the eigenspectrum of the target correlation matrix, which gives each
target unit variance. Because target correlation is invariant to column offsets, correlating
$\Gamma$ is identical to subtracting only $\bar X_{i\cdot}$ before calculating target
correlations. We call this operation within-ligand centering and use full two-way centering
when discussing the score-scale residual matrix. Correlation-spectrum PR was calculated by
Eq.~\ref{eq:pr}. Entropy effective rank, PC1 fraction and the number of components required
for 90\% variance were secondary summaries \cite{mazzucato2016,roy2007}.

Covariance-spectrum PR retained target-specific variances. Docking-44 missingness
sensitivities used target-mean and target-median imputation, complete rows, and an additive
least-squares fit using observed cells only; missing residuals were set to zero after fitting
row and column effects in the latter analysis. Positive-score clipping was removed in a
DOCKSTRING sensitivity.

The row-norm-preserving null sampled independent Gaussian directions in the
$(P-1)$-dimensional target-zero-sum subspace and rescaled every simulated row to its observed
residual Euclidean norm before column standardization. Five hundred draws were evaluated on
fixed 12,000-ligand supports. Additive Gaussian, empirical target-column permutation and
transformation-matched parallel-analysis nulls are reported in Supplementary Information.
All apply the same centering and standardization operator to observed and null matrices.

\subsection{Physicochemical slopes and residual-PC1 interpretation}

RDKit molecular weight, heavy-atom count and rotatable-bond count were calculated from the
analysis SMILES. Docking-44 used all 12,651 mean-imputed ligands. For DOCKSTRING, descriptor
analysis used a score-blind simple-random sample of 15,000 complete ligands selected with
NumPy seed 71; the full score matrix was not required for this interpretation. For each
target we fitted
\begin{equation}
X_{ij}=\beta_{0j}+\beta_{Mj}(\mathrm{MW}_i/100)
       +\beta_{Rj}\mathrm{RotB}_i+\varepsilon_{ij}.
\end{equation}
We report the distributions of $\beta_{Mj}$ and $\beta_{Rj}$, the cosine between the
target-wise molecular-weight residual-slope vector and residual-PC1 loadings, and the
absolute correlation between molecular weight and residual-PC1 ligand scores. Component
signs were oriented only for reporting. A Docking-44 sensitivity removed 4mqs, which
contained 3,352 of the 8,159 imputed cells. RDKit rotatable bonds are a molecular proxy and
were not treated as a decomposition of Vina's internal torsional term.

The chemical-group-held-out predictive decomposition used seven descriptors in fixed order:
heavy-atom count, molecular weight, Labute ASA, TPSA, cLogP, rotatable-bond count and ring
count. Five-fold GroupKFold split the frozen Docking-44 Butina groups or DOCKSTRING Murcko
groups; each acyclic DOCKSTRING molecule was a singleton group. Target offsets, residual
target scales, descriptor means and scales, and the target-specific linear coefficients
were re-estimated within every training fold. We pooled the out-of-fold residual score
errors, recalculated their target correlations and PR, and compared mean squared target
correlation before and after descriptor removal. Residual-PC1 $R^2$ used pooled descriptive
loadings, but every ligand-level prediction remained group held out. This analysis measures
predictable score structure and has no causal interpretation.

\subsection{Cross-scorer sensitivity on fixed Vina-selected poses}

To test whether the shared-axis pattern persists when the same poses are evaluated by other
functions, we held the released DOCKSTRING Vina-selected top poses fixed and changed only the
scoring function. A simple random subset of \TransportLigands{} ligands
(seed 20260802) from the frozen 15,000-ligand support was taken across the nine targets whose
pose archives are published, giving \TransportCells{} cells. Poses were scored with smina
using the Vina and Vinardo functions, and with ODDT implementations of RF-Score v1--v3,
NNScore 2.0 and a pretrained linear PLECscore; RF-Score and NNScore models were trained
offline on the bundled PDBbind 2016 descriptor tables. Scores were not clipped, because
clipping only the Vina-family columns would be asymmetric across scorers. For each function
we computed the raw and within-ligand-centred correlation spectra, PR, PC1 fraction, mean
squared off-diagonal correlation and the cosine of the PC1 loading vector with the uniform
target direction, with 95\% cluster-bootstrap intervals. The four criteria were stated before the
non-Vina scores were inspected, but that ordering is not independently verifiable from the
released package and the criteria should be read as a descriptive checklist rather than a
preregistered test. This is a rescoring of retained Vina poses, not an independent
re-docking: a function that would select different poses during its own search is not
simulated, so the analysis estimates scorer sensitivity conditional on Vina pose selection.
RF-Score v3 additionally consumes Vina terms as input features and is therefore not
function-independent, and all three machine-learned scorers are trained on crystal complexes
and applied off-distribution to docked poses. These criteria are weak: a broadly positive
score matrix can have a near-uniform leading vector without establishing a chemically useful
shared axis. The secondary ODDT stage ran under a separate Python~3.9/ODDT~0.7 environment;
its released outputs are checksum-verified but are not recreated by the default Python~3.12
container or \texttt{make all} target.

\subsection{Broad DOCKSTRING--ChEMBL ligand-wise benchmark}

ChEMBL activities for the 31 human single-protein targets mapped to DOCKSTRING were
refetched without a potency floor or assay-type restriction. The broad primary contract
required non-null pChEMBL, exact standard relation and endpoint Ki, Kd, IC50 or EC50. The
source pull contained 212,254 records; endpoint filtering retained 196,889, and removal of
146 records with unparseable structures left 196,743 for aggregation. Ligands were matched
to DOCKSTRING by RDKit standard full InChIKey, repeated records were median-aggregated per
ligand--target cell, and ligands observed on at least two targets were retained. A less
assay-heterogeneous, endpoint-restricted sensitivity required a human binding assay and
endpoint Ki or Kd. Its within-ligand comparisons may still pair a Ki-derived cell with a
Kd-derived cell. Separate Ki,
Kd, IC50 and EC50 arms exposed rather than averaged endpoint heterogeneity; no target pair
in those arms mixes endpoint types.

Every docking transformation was fitted outside the complete evaluated ligand set. Target
means, target standard deviations, residual target standard deviations and the grand mean
came from the remaining complete DOCKSTRING reference; the evaluated ligand's row mean used
all 58 docking targets. Representations were absolute Vina, target-centred unscaled,
two-way-centred unscaled, column-standardized, target-centred residual-scaled and scaled
two-way residual scores. A docking target prior repeated the reference target means. An
external ChEMBL prior used target means after excluding every evaluated ligand, and a
benchmark-fitted cohort prior recomputed target means after excluding the held-out ligand's
entire Bemis--Murcko cluster, falling back to the external prior when necessary. The last is
a diagnostic of assay-selection and target-prevalence structure, not a deployable
ligand-specific predictor.

For every ligand, all experimentally observed unordered target pairs were compared; an
experimental tie was excluded and a docking-score tie received half credit. Mean
concordance gave each evaluated ligand equal weight. Paired differences were resampled 5,000
times by ligand, intact Murcko scaffold and intact Butina cluster. Butina clustering used
radius-2, 2,048-bit Morgan fingerprints and Tanimoto 0.65; the conservative chemical
interval is the union of the Murcko and Butina intervals. Delete-one-target jackknifing
masked one experimental target at a time and reevaluated the already externally fitted score
representations; it is reported separately as an evaluation-panel composition sensitivity.
Chemical intervals remain conditional on the fixed target labels, while the jackknife is not
a population confidence interval for all proteins.

\subsection{Rank-matched ligand-feature and nuisance controls}

We repeated the group-held-out decomposition on the DOCKSTRING reference filtered to remove
structural overlap with the three structure-annotated panels and used for
the fixed-panel comparison (\DescriptorGeometryLigands{} ligands after excluding every
connectivity block occurring in DAVIS, PKIS2 or PKIS1) and the same ordered 20 kinases. Every
arm used the identical five scaffold folds, fold-local target offsets and scales, feature
standardization and separate least-squares coefficient vector for each target. Predictive
performance was summarized by mean out-of-fold target $R^2$ and by the operational reduction
in mean squared target correlation after subtracting the fixed out-of-fold prediction.

All predictive bases had seven dimensions. The primary physicochemical basis contained the
seven descriptors above. The capacity-matched chemical control contained one total Morgan-
environment-count coordinate and six random projections of unnormalised radius-2, 2,048-bin
Morgan count fingerprints. The first of 20 consecutively seeded projections was locked as
the representative before their performance was inspected; the full seed range is an
algorithmic sensitivity, not an uncertainty interval. Two unit-normalised bit-fingerprint
projections tested the effect of removing count magnitude. A stable nuisance basis mapped
each exact input SMILES deterministically to seven centred BLAKE2b-derived coordinates.
Twenty seeded row permutations of the standardised physicochemical matrix preserved its
dimension and feature covariance while destroying ligand--score association. An
outcome-derived transported rank-7 SVD reconstruction was retained only as a nonpredictive
subspace reference, not a capacity ceiling.

For each arm we also correlated the upper triangle of its predicted-component target
correlation matrix with the four fixed experimental maps. Equation~\ref{eq:nuisanceprojection}
states the relevant projection algebra. With a shared nuisance basis, independently fitted
target coefficients can inherit a low-rank sketch of the training target covariance even
when held-out predictive performance is negligible; subsequent column standardisation
removes the predicted columns' scale. Component-map concordance was therefore interpreted
only beside the out-of-fold performance metrics and the nuisance ensemble. Chemical-group block reweighting
held the fitted out-of-fold surfaces and fixed target panel constant; its ranges are
conditional sensitivity intervals, not chemical- or target-population confidence intervals.

\subsection{Within-Vina fixed-pose term attribution}

To describe which AutoDock Vina terms carry covariance in a fixed-pose subset, we rescored a
fixed pose block term by term. A simple random subset of 512
ligands (seed 20260802) from the frozen 15,000-ligand DOCKSTRING support was taken across
the nine targets whose published pose archives were available, giving 4,608 cells. Published
top poses were converted to PDBQT with Open Babel 3.1.0 using Gasteiger charges and explicit
hydrogens, then rescored against the released receptor files. The roundtrip reproduced the
released scores for 99.6\% of cells within 0.10~kcal/mol at cell-level Pearson 0.9996. We
then computed the correlation spectrum, PR, PC1 fraction and mean squared target correlation
for each individual Vina term, before and after within-ligand centring, and attributed each
term's share of the shared row-axis covariance. Original docked torsion trees were not
published, so this is an attribution of retained poses rather than a causal re-docking
ablation.

\subsection{Target-pair geometry and the factorial estimand comparison}

For matrix $X$, target-pair geometry was
\begin{equation}
G(X)=\operatorname{corr}_{\mathrm{ligands}}(X),
\end{equation}
and $G_R(X)=G(\Gamma)$. Agreement between two geometries was Spearman correlation between
their $P(P-1)/2$ upper-triangular edges. This statistic describes co-response across the
specified ligand supports; it is not a compound-level affinity correlation.

For every experimental panel we computed the four concordances
\begin{equation}
\rho\{G(X),G(E)\},\quad \rho\{G(X),G_R(E)\},\quad
\rho\{G_R(X),G(E)\},\quad \rho\{G_R(X),G_R(E)\}.
\end{equation}
The docking-side increment against the fixed raw endpoint was the third minus the first,
and the increment against the fixed centred endpoint was the fourth minus the second. For
each fixed endpoint, a paired QAP jointly relabelled the targets of raw and centred docking
geometries, held the experimental geometry fixed, and recomputed the difference for 49,999
permutations \cite{hubert1976,dekker2007}.

A separate transformation-matched null used one score-blind simple-random sample without
replacement of 15,000 ligands from the 259,579-ligand, experimental-structure-excluded
DOCKSTRING reference. Within each of 2,000 draws, ligand identities were independently
permuted inside every docking target column; raw and within-ligand-centred docking maps were
then derived from the identical permuted matrix while the selected raw or centred
experimental geometry remained fixed. This preserves docking target marginals, variances,
ties and clipped zeros while destroying ligand-wise target coordination. Each probability
compared the observed increment calculated on that same fixed chemical support with its
null increments; the full-reference increment is reported descriptively and was not
compared directly with the subsampled null distribution.

The sequential experimental-first fraction divided
$\rho\{G(X),G_R(E)\}-\rho\{G(X),G(E)\}$ by the joint raw/raw-to-centred/centred change.
Because this quantity is order dependent, we also reported the reverse path, the two-factor
interaction on the Spearman scale and Shapley-style path-averaged marginal contributions.
The latter average each transformation's marginal contribution across both orders and are
descriptive rather than causal.

\subsection{Cross-panel experimental geometry and transformation-matched nulls}

Cross-panel agreement was the mean of six pairwise Spearman correlations among the
four experimental geometries. A global network QAP fixed DAVIS labels and independently
permuted complete target labels for PKIS2, PKIS1 and KiRHub, applying identical label
permutations to raw and centred geometries. In a separate transformation-matched null,
ligand identities were independently permuted within every target column of every panel;
raw and centred maps were then recomputed from the same permuted matrices. This preserves
target marginals, variances, ties and ceiling patterns while destroying ligand-wise target
coordination. Two thousand matched draws evaluated whether the observed centred-minus-raw
change exceeded the mechanical change induced by the projection.

The fixed target-pair endpoint comprised pairs in the centred upper 10\% in at least two of
DAVIS, PKIS2 and PKIS1, giving 15 positives among 190 pairs. KiRHub was not used to define
the labels. AUROC and average precision were calculated for KiRHub, sequence, pocket and
docking predictors; target-label QAPs permuted complete target identities rather than
treating 190 dyads as independent observations.

\subsection{Protein-sequence and KLIFS controls}

Receptor-domain sequences were extracted from the exact DOCKSTRING PDBQT receptor files and
globally aligned with BLOSUM62 to obtain pairwise sequence identity. KLIFS pocket identity
was calculated from the aligned 85-residue human kinase-pocket sequences in a checksum-
anchored KLIFS snapshot \cite{vanlinden2014,kooistra2016}. Partial Spearman statistics rank-transformed the docking,
experimental, sequence and pocket edge vectors and residualized predictor and endpoint on
sequence plus pocket ranks before correlating the residuals. Unrestricted QAP used 50,000
complete target-label permutations and refit the partial statistic for each draw.

The strict sensitivity restricted label exchanges to KLIFS groups. The panel contained 11
TK targets, three AGC targets, three CMGC targets and one target each in STE, CAMK and an
other group. One hundred thousand Monte Carlo permutations were drawn from the
1,437,004,800 possible within-group relabellings. Singleton targets remain fixed under this
scheme. The restriction is a conditional sensitivity, not proof that targets are
exchangeable within each group.

\subsection{Chemical support, centering panel and map recovery}

Chemical-support analysis formed lower- and upper-molecular-weight groups at the empirical
25th- and 75th-percentile thresholds, retaining every boundary tie, and compared the
resulting centred target geometries. One thousand random
disjoint splits of the same sizes formed a matched null. Murcko and Butina cluster
bootstraps were reported separately. Butina clustering used RDKit Morgan radius-2,
2,048-bit fingerprints and a Tanimoto similarity threshold of 0.65; an entire cluster and
all of its ligands formed the resampling unit \cite{morgan1965,rogers2010,butina1999,rdkit2025}.

An exploratory docking-domain analysis applied the same inclusive lower/upper-threshold split to the
full Docking-44 support and a fixed, score-blind 15,000-ligand DOCKSTRING support and
computed Spearman agreement between the strict upper triangles of the two residual
target-correlation maps. Paired cluster bootstrap resampled the union of
chemical groups 300 times. Five hundred simple-random disjoint support pairs calibrated
finite-sample agreement. A separate 200-repeat control assigned complete chemical groups to
two halves within deciles of group-median molecular weight, greedily balancing row counts;
this produced chemically disjoint supports with closely matched molecular-weight
distributions. To distinguish genuine between-band change from instability caused merely by
restricting chemical support, we also split the low- and high-MW bands separately. Within
each band, 500 equal-size row-random disjoint splits and 200 MW-stratified
whole-chemical-group-disjoint splits were evaluated on both the raw and row-centred
surfaces. Their central 95\% repeated-split ranges describe composition sensitivity, not
population confidence intervals. We repeated the low/high comparison after the group-held-out seven-descriptor
decomposition and across six independently seeded DOCKSTRING supports. A post hoc descriptor-
family extension repeated this quantile-threshold split, with boundary ties retained, for heavy-atom count, molecular weight,
Labute ASA, TPSA, cLogP, rotatable-bond count and ring count. Because several of these
descriptors are correlated, it characterizes the specificity of the observed support shift
rather than testing seven independent mechanisms. These comparisons test transfer between
observed molecular-size-defined domains, not a molecular-weight-specific causal effect.

The centering-panel sensitivity used the identical 259,579-by-58 DOCKSTRING reference.
Under the local contract, the 20 kinase columns were extracted and each ligand mean was
calculated over those columns. Under the global contract, the row mean was calculated over
all 58 targets before the same 20 columns were extracted. Both were compared with the same
centred experimental matrices using 49,999 paired target-label permutations.

For sampling recovery, ligand subsets of 50, 100, 200, 500, 1,000, 2,000 and 5,000 were
drawn without replacement from each large matrix. After within-ligand centering, the subset
target map was compared with the full source-library map. Each size used 100 draws. Oracle
Approximating Shrinkage was a secondary small-sample estimator \cite{chen2010}. A chemical-group-held-out analysis formed a map from the sampled ligands and
compared it with a map calculated after excluding their Murcko or acyclic-connectivity
groups. Recovery ranges describe repeated samples from the observed library, not a target
population or a shifted chemical distribution.

\subsection{Mixed-family censored sensitivity}

The primary Secondary Pharmacology Database analysis used 12 mapped target groups and 59
of their 66 possible target pairs; alternative exact-only and common-rectangle estimands
used smaller supports \cite{sutherland2023}. Released IC50 bounds were analyzed as percentile-ranked bounds and as
censor-aware binary outcomes at 10 and 30~$\mu$M. Pairwise-complete experimental
correlations retained censoring metadata. Unrestricted and family-preserving target-label
permutations were evaluated separately. The source subset is 91.6\% right-censored, so this
analysis was not combined with the dense kinase panels.

\subsection{Dense and sparse observed-pair sensitivities}

For dense operational checks, full Standard InChIKeys were recomputed with the pinned RDKit
version from the released DOCKSTRING, DAVIS and PKIS2 SMILES. Duplicate raw DOCKSTRING
profiles and duplicate experimental identities were collapsed cell-wise by the median. The
21 shared kinase targets were the fixed 20-target geometry panel plus PTK2/FAK. All
DOCKSTRING rows whose recomputed 14-character Standard-InChI connectivity block occurred in
the corresponding evaluation panel were removed before fitting target means, target standard
deviations, the grand mean or residual target standard deviations. Positive reference and
evaluation Vina scores were clipped to zero under the primary contract.

The DAVIS primary estimand was the equal-ligand mean of within-ligand pairwise concordance on
the dense 59-by-21 exact-key block. Lower Vina scores predicted higher pKd; exact pKd ties and
pairs for which both measurements were at the released pKd-5 floor were excluded. The PKIS2
estimand used the dense 154-by-21 block and retained experimental target differences greater
than 10 percentage points; higher single-dose inhibition/displacement and lower Vina score
indicated preference. Predicted score ties received half credit. These are observed-target
ordering checks, not retrieval of unmeasured targets; PKIS2 is not an affinity measurement.

Representations were absolute Vina, target-centred unscaled, column-standardized,
target-centred divided by the reference residual target SD, and the scaled two-way residual.
Every evaluation-ligand row mean used by the dense scaled residual rule was calculated over
the same 21 shared targets; all target offsets and scales came from the leakage-free reference.
The ligand-invariant docking target prior assigned every evaluation ligand the target means
fitted on the same leakage-free DOCKSTRING reference. Paired contrasts included absolute
Vina minus this prior and the full path from absolute scores through target-offset removal,
residual-SD scaling and ligand-row correction.
For the last two representations, Eq.~\ref{eq:scalinginteraction} follows directly from the
score definitions. Uncertainty used 5,000 ligand, Murcko/connectivity-cluster and Butina-
cluster bootstraps with seed 0. Acyclic Murcko groups were identified by recomputed
Standard-InChI connectivity block; Butina used radius-2, 2,048-bit Morgan fingerprints and
Tanimoto similarity 0.65. Delete-one-target analyses refitted every offset, row mean and scale
on the remaining 20-target reference; their normal intervals are descriptive composition
sensitivities because the 21 targets are fixed and non-random.

DAVIS outcome-conditioned sensitivities separated pairs with two uncensored pKd values from
floor-versus-uncensored pairs. The primary PKIS2 sensitivity required both target values to be at
least 65\% inhibition/displacement and retained the aggregate margin of more than 10
percentage points; a secondary sensitivity relaxed only that margin to exact non-ties.
These strata were selected after examining the aggregate
results, and the transformation-path and stratum intervals were not adjusted for
multiplicity. They are reported as mechanistic and hypothesis-generating decompositions,
not alternative primary endpoints.

The original Docking-44--ChEMBL benchmark was retained only as a sparse support sensitivity
and was extracted from ChEMBL release 34
\cite{zdrazil2024,chembl34}. The primary block retained exact standard relations, aggregated
repeated compound--target measurements by the median and included Ki, Kd, IC50 and EC50.
Only experimentally observed cells were evaluated; no missing activity was imputed. For
each ligand and unordered pair of observed targets, a prediction was correct when the more
negative docking score corresponded to the larger pChEMBL value. Experimental ties were
excluded, predicted ties received half credit, and per-ligand pair accuracy was averaged so
that ligands received equal weight.

A pair-weighted sensitivity instead gave every non-tied comparison equal weight. The
ligand-identity null permuted complete docking-score rows relative to the experimental
profiles. In 25 chemical-de-redundancy repeats, one ligand was sampled from each Murcko
cluster before 200 such row permutations; the resulting probability distribution is a
sensitivity across representative supports rather than a single cluster-level test.

For this sparse sensitivity only, all docking transformation parameters were estimated from the 12,514 Docking-44 ligands
outside the 137-ligand evaluation cohort. Representations included absolute Vina,
target-centred unscaled, two-way-centred unscaled, column-standardized and residual-
standardized scores. The last representation subtracts row and column effects on the score
scale and divides each target by its externally estimated residual standard deviation. Its
ligand row mean was calculated over all 44 Docking-44 targets before the 38 targets observed
in the ChEMBL benchmark were extracted; this 44-target centering panel is part of the
operational score definition.
Equation~\ref{eq:rankinvariance} was checked numerically by comparing target-centred and
two-way-centred unscaled scores.

Target-only controls separated ligand-specific ranking from target marginals. The docking
prior repeated each target's mean clipped Vina score from the 12,514-ligand external
reference for every evaluation ligand. An external experimental prior analogously used
targetwise mean pChEMBL from 96,744 ChEMBL ligands after excluding the evaluation cohort.
The cohort outcome prior was cross-fitted within the 137-ligand benchmark: for each ligand,
targetwise means were recomputed after excluding its complete Bemis--Murcko scaffold
cluster, with absent targets falling back to the external experimental prior. This final
control is target-only and scaffold-held-out but outcome-informed and cohort-specific; it is
not an externally deployable predictor. Coverage strata evaluated the already cross-fitted
predictions without refitting them.

A one-factor centering-panel sensitivity recomputed each evaluation ligand's row mean over
the 38 evaluated targets. Per-target missing-score means, target offsets, the training grand
mean and residual standard deviations were held at their original estimates from the
12,514-ligand, 44-target reference. This isolates dependence on the six row-mean-only
context targets; it is not a newly fitted 38-target normalization. Paired ligand, Murcko and
Butina bootstraps used the same 5,000-replicate contract as the primary comparison.

Sparse-sensitivity uncertainty used 5,000 ligand bootstraps, 5,000 Murcko-cluster bootstraps and 5,000
Butina-cluster bootstraps; conservative intervals are unions of the two chemical-cluster
intervals. Leave-one-target-out values describe composition sensitivity conditional on the
observed 38-target panel. Human binding Ki/Kd, same-endpoint, minimum activity-difference,
minimum target-coverage and measurement-discordance analyses were secondary. Equivalence readings of these
intervals are post hoc throughout: no margin was preregistered, and every margin reported
here was chosen after the corresponding benchmark had been computed. We therefore report a
margin sensitivity curve rather than a single threshold, and treat the paired difference and
its interval as the primary result.

\section{Results}

\subsection{A shared ligand axis dominates both Vina matrices}

The column-correlation spectrum of Docking-44 was strongly concentrated. Its first
principal component (PC1) explained \DffPCOne{} of variance and its PR effective dimension
was \DffRawPR{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:spectral}a). DOCKSTRING-58 gave the same qualitative
pattern at larger scale: PC1 explained \DsPCOne{} and PR dimension was \DsRawPR{}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:spectral}b). The PC1 loading vectors were close to a uniform target vector
(cosines \DffUniformCosine{} and \DsUniformCosine{}); 43 of 44 and 57 of 58 loadings had
the same sign. PC1 scores correlated 0.998 and 0.997 with the mean of the
column-standardized scores for each ligand. The dominant mode was therefore a shared
ligand-wide score axis rather than one target or protein family.

For a $P$-target correlation matrix $C$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_k$, PR has the exact form
\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{PR}}
=\frac{(\sum_k\lambda_k)^2}{\sum_k\lambda_k^2}
=\frac{P}{1+(P-1)\overline{r_{ij}^{\,2}}},
\label{eq:pr}
\end{equation}
where the bar is over unique off-diagonal target pairs. PR is thus a monotone
re-expression of mean squared inter-target correlation on an effective-component scale; it
is not an independent statistic, an algebraic rank or a docking-quality score.

Removing the mean score of each ligand increased PR to \DffResidualPR{} and
\DsResidualPR{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:spectral}a--c), and turned the predominantly positive
correlations of the uncentred matrices into roughly balanced positive and negative ones. The
residual maps nevertheless remain strongly dependent, and the useful way to say how strongly
is as an effect size rather than a test. Randomizing each ligand's residual direction within
the target-zero-sum subspace, while preserving its residual norm, gives PR near the
algebraic ceilings of 43 and 57 (medians \DffRowNull{} and \DsRowNull{} on fixed
12,000-ligand supports). The observed residual PR values of
\DffCommonNullObservedPR{} and \DsCommonNullObservedPR{} therefore
represent a compression of roughly 43 effectively independent target directions into 9, and
of 57 into 18. Because that null removes aligned target structure apart from the zero-sum
and row-norm constraints, its rejection at
$N=12{,}000$ is a foregone conclusion; the informative quantity is the compression ratio, not
the probability. Supplementary Information reports the full battery of nulls.

The direction, but not the magnitude, of the PR increase after centring was robust to
choosing a correlation or covariance spectrum. Covariance-spectrum PR increased from
\DffRawCovariancePR{} to \DffResidualCovariancePR{} in Docking-44 and from
\DsRawCovariancePR{} to \DsResidualCovariancePR{} in DOCKSTRING-58; the lower covariance residual values
show that the magnitude depends on whether target variances are standardized. The direction
was also robust to missing-data handling and clipping:
Docking-44 residual correlation PR was \DffResidualPR{}, \DffMedianImputedResidualPR{} and
\DffObservedALSResidualPR{} under target-mean imputation,
target-median imputation and observed-cell additive least squares; and retaining the rare
positive DOCKSTRING scores instead of clipping them moved residual PR from \DsResidualPR{} only to
\DsUnclippedResidualPR{}. Complete cases gave \DffCompleteCaseResidualPR{}, but the 9,297 complete ligands are a chemically selected
subset. Full missingness, covariance, clipping, target-subsampling and chemical-cluster
sensitivities are in Supplementary Information.

Broad protein-family labels did not resolve the remaining target dependence: the
within-minus-between-family differences in mean absolute residual correlation were 0.030 and
0.017, with label-permutation $p=0.073$ and 0.067 for Docking-44 and DOCKSTRING-58,
respectively (Supplementary Fig.~S3). The residual map is therefore correlated, but is not
demonstrated here to encode broad family organization.

What survives centring has a descriptive ligand-property association. Per-target regressions of
Vina score on molecular weight and RDKit rotatable-bond count gave median conditional
molecular-weight slopes of \DffConditionalMWSlope{} and \DsConditionalMWSlope{} kcal/mol per
100~Da and median rotatable-bond slopes of $+\DffConditionalRotSlope{}$ and
$+\DsConditionalRotSlope{}$ kcal/mol per bond, the flexibility slope being positive for
\DffPositiveRotTargets{}/44 and \DsPositiveRotTargets{}/58 targets. Target-wise
molecular-weight residual slopes aligned with residual-PC1 loadings (cosines
\DffMWResidualLoadingCosine{} and \DsMWResidualLoadingCosine{}). Row centring therefore
removes the average ligand-size tendency but keeps the target-to-target heterogeneity in how
each score responds to size and flexibility. That heterogeneity is compatible with scoring
bias, pocket-dependent physicochemical preference, or both; this analysis does not
distinguish them. These regressions are descriptive: RDKit rotatable-bond count is not
Vina's internal torsional term. Docking-44
used the full mean-imputed support, with the alignment persisting after excluding 4mqs, the
target with the most missing scores; DOCKSTRING used one fixed, score-blind 15,000-ligand
descriptor support (Supplementary Fig.~S5).

Five-fold chemical-group-held-out fits confirmed that low-dimensional ligand features
have non-zero held-out predictive performance for the residual score surface: removing seven ordinary descriptors reduced
mean squared target correlation by \DffDescriptorCorrelationReduction{} and
\DsDescriptorCorrelationReduction{}. These non-additive operational ratios are not shares
of correlation explained. The rank-matched controls below show that the predictive basis is
not specific to those descriptors and that correlation geometry of a fitted component has a
separate scale-free nuisance-projection failure. Ligand-property controls serve the same
bounding role in structure-based benchmark design
\cite{sieg2019,wallach2018,chen2019dude,gabel2014}.

A secondary fixed-pose sensitivity is reported in Supplementary Information. On
\TransportLigands{} ligands and \TransportTargets{} targets, five of six non-Vina scorers
met the stated shared-axis criteria, whereas PLECscore-linear falsified a universal-threshold
claim; all functions were evaluated on Vina-selected poses. A signed within-Vina attribution
on 512 ligands found that gauss2 contributed \VinaGaussTwoSharedAxisShare{} of the shared-axis covariance and
\VinaGaussTwoResidualInnerProductShare{} of
the residual Frobenius inner product, but deletion was evaluated arithmetically on fixed
poses. Neither analysis is an independent redocking comparison, a non-Vina replication of
pose selection or a causal term-ablation experiment.

The two score matrices were generated in separate projects and computational pipelines, not
sampled as statistically independent observations. Two authors of the present study also
contributed to the Docking-44 source study.
They shared \SharedFullInchiKeys{} full Standard InChIKeys,
\SharedConnectivityBlocks{} connectivity blocks and \SharedMurckoScaffolds{} non-empty
Bemis--Murcko scaffolds. Their target lists contained \SharedTargetIdentities{} mapped
protein identities, of which \SharedReceptorStructures{} used the same receptor structure.
The replicated spectral pattern should therefore be read as agreement across two different
data-generation pipelines with quantified overlap.

\subsection{Exploratory molecular-size splits alter the map, which is recoverable within each source library}

Target-pair geometry is a property of a target panel evaluated over a specified chemical
library. In exploratory, post hoc analyses within the observed source libraries, this dependence was pronounced on the full Docking-44 support and a fixed
15,000-ligand DOCKSTRING support. Residual maps from the lowest and highest molecular-weight
quartiles correlated only \DffMWDomainRho{} (chemical-group bootstrap
\DffMWDomainLow{}--\DffMWDomainHigh{}) in Docking-44 and \DsMWDomainRho{}
(\DsMWDomainLow{}--\DsMWDomainHigh{}) in DOCKSTRING-58, compared with
\DffMWDomainControl{} and \DsMWDomainControl{} for chemically disjoint controls with
closely matched molecular-weight distributions (Fig.~\ref{fig:support}a). The same
between-band shift was already present before row centring: raw-map agreements were
\DffMWRawDomainRho{} (\DffMWRawDomainLow{}--\DffMWRawDomainHigh{}) and
\DsMWRawDomainRho{} (\DsMWRawDomainLow{}--\DsMWRawDomainHigh{}), versus
\DffMWRawDomainControl{} and \DsMWRawDomainControl{} in their MW-matched controls. Row
centring therefore did not manufacture the domain shift.

Restriction-matched controls excluded a second mechanical explanation. When each extreme
MW band was split internally while keeping entire chemical groups disjoint, residual maps
agreed at \DffMWWithinLowResidualGroupDisjointMean{} and
\DffMWWithinHighResidualGroupDisjointMean{} in the low and high Docking-44 bands, and at
\DsMWWithinLowResidualGroupDisjointMean{} and
\DsMWWithinHighResidualGroupDisjointMean{} in DOCKSTRING-58; even the lowest endpoint of
the four repeated-split ranges was \DsMWWithinHighResidualGroupDisjointLow{}.
Corresponding raw-map means were
\DffMWWithinLowRawGroupDisjointMean{}/\DffMWWithinHighRawGroupDisjointMean{} and
\DsMWWithinLowRawGroupDisjointMean{}/\DsMWWithinHighRawGroupDisjointMean{}.
Thus correlation maps were reproducible within either restricted band while differing
sharply between bands. Target-pair signs
changed for \DffMWDomainSignFlip{} and \DsMWDomainSignFlip{} of edges. Removing the
seven-feature component in strictly group-held-out folds increased cross-domain agreement to
\DffMWDomainAdjusted{} and \DsMWDomainAdjusted{}, respectively, but did not approach the
matched-domain controls. Size-matched random disjoint supports agreed at
\DffMWDomainRandomControl{} and \DsMWDomainRandomControl{}, respectively. Across six
independently seeded DOCKSTRING supports the unadjusted cross-domain value remained between
\DsMWDomainSeedLow{} and \DsMWDomainSeedHigh{}. Across post hoc extreme splits of the seven inspected descriptors, the
three strongest shifts in both matrices formed a correlated molecular-size family: Labute
ASA, molecular weight and heavy-atom count gave residual-map agreements of
\DffSizeFamilyRhoLow{}--\DffSizeFamilyRhoHigh{} in Docking-44 and
\DsSizeFamilyRhoLow{}--\DsSizeFamilyRhoHigh{} in DOCKSTRING-58. The remaining descriptor
splits were weaker (\DffOtherDescriptorRhoLow{}--\DffOtherDescriptorRhoHigh{} and
\DsOtherDescriptorRhoLow{}--\DsOtherDescriptorRhoHigh{}, respectively), although all lay
below the same dataset-level MW-matched composition reference rather than a descriptor-
matched null. Residual PR itself moved in opposite directions across the MW split:
\DffMWLowDomainResidualPR{} to \DffMWHighDomainResidualPR{} in Docking-44, but
\DsMWLowDomainResidualPR{} to \DsMWHighDomainResidualPR{} in DOCKSTRING-58. These post hoc
analyses distinguish molecular-size-domain drift from finite
sample size or generic chemical-group disjointness; molecular weight defines the tested
domains but is not established as the sole cause, and the PR reversal precludes a monotone
rank-quality interpretation.

Experimental maps showed the same qualitative boundary. Splitting compounds by
molecular-weight quartile changed the map more than matched random disjoint splits in PKIS2
and PKIS1: low- versus high-MW geometry agreement was 0.333 and 0.547, compared with
random-split means of 0.845 and 0.833 ($p=0.001$ for each; Fig.~\ref{fig:support}b). DAVIS,
with only 18 compounds in each extreme quartile, gave 0.531 against a random mean of 0.619
($p=0.173$). The direction of individual edge changes was not consistent across panels, so
molecular weight is a support marker rather than an identified mechanism.

The target set used to compute each ligand mean had a modest effect. Computing row means
over the local 20 kinases and over all 58 DOCKSTRING targets before extracting the same 20
produced maps with Spearman correlation 0.973. Concordance with the experimental panels
changed by $-0.008$ to $-0.019$, with no resolved positive all-58 gain (paired
$p=0.678$--$0.925$; Supplementary Information). The local 20-target definition is primary
because it matches the validation panel.

Although conditional, each observed source-library map was closely reproduced by far fewer ligands
than the full matrix. Across 100 simple-random replicates of 200 same-distribution ligands,
mean agreement with the full map was \DsProbeTwoHundred{} for DOCKSTRING-58
(2.5--97.5\% range 0.885--0.938) and \DffProbeTwoHundred{} for Docking-44
(0.898--0.962). At 500 ligands the means were \DsProbeFiveHundred{} and
\DffProbeFiveHundred{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:support}c). When sampled chemical groups were
excluded from the reference map, 200-ligand agreement remained 0.916 for DOCKSTRING-58 and
0.927 for Docking-44 (Fig.~\ref{fig:support}d). These panels require 11,600 rather than
15,083,480 score cells for DOCKSTRING and 8,800 rather than 556,644 for Docking-44,
arithmetic reductions of approximately 1,300-fold and 63-fold.

The 2.5--97.5\% replicate ranges at 200 ligands were 0.053 wide for DOCKSTRING-58 and 0.064
for Docking-44, comparable to or larger than the 0.001--0.045 docking-side increments in the
fixed centred-endpoint factorial.
Thus 200 ligands supported coarse reconstruction and support-shift diagnosis here, but not
resolution of small between-map or method contrasts.

This is a descriptive, support-specific sampling-reliability result. It shows that the target-pair map of a specified
scoring-function, receptor and chemical-library combination can be measured without docking
the entire source library. It does not show that the map is biologically correct, that 200
ligands recover individual target profiles, or that the estimate transfers to a shifted
chemical library. Nor does it establish 200 ligands or any observed agreement value as a
universal accuracy threshold for a different library, receptor panel or scorer.

\subsection{Centred experimental geometries agree across panels, but the increment is
mechanical}

We defined target-pair geometry as the upper triangle of the target correlation matrix.
Experimental matrices were restricted to the same ordered 20 kinases and contained 72
DAVIS compounds, 645 PKIS2 compounds, 360 PKIS1 compounds and 92 KiRHub compounds. The
panels differ in compounds, assay technologies and endpoints; they are separately published
assay panels rather than independent samples from a target superpopulation. DAVIS is also
strongly measurement-limited: \DavisFloorCells{} of 1,440 cells
(\DavisFloorPercent{}) lie at the 10,000-nM Kd upper bound, corresponding to a pKd floor
of 5. A binary above-floor-status geometry correlated
$\rho=\DavisStatusGeometryRho{}$ with its continuous pKd geometry; centred DOCKSTRING
remained associated with that binary geometry
($\rho=\DavisStatusDockingRho{}$, $p=\DavisStatusDockingP{}$).
Among the three panels with molecular structures, pairwise intersections contained only
\DavisPkisTwoConnectivityOverlap{}, \DavisPkisOneConnectivityOverlap{} and
\PkisTwoPkisOneConnectivityOverlap{} Standard-InChI connectivity blocks for
DAVIS--PKIS2, DAVIS--PKIS1 and PKIS2--PKIS1, respectively, with
\ExperimentalTripleConnectivityOverlap{} shared by all three. KiRHub structures were not
available, so its remaining chemical overlap could not be audited at structure or scaffold
level.

The four within-ligand-centred experimental geometries agreed strongly. Their mean pairwise
Spearman correlation was \CrossPanelCentered{}, compared with \CrossPanelRaw{} for the
uncentred geometries (Supplementary Table~S10). KiRHub, inspected after
a 15-pair endpoint
had been fixed from the other three panels, correlated 0.834 with their mean centred
geometry. The value was \KiRHubOverlapExcludedRho{} after removing the
\KiRHubDavisNameOverlap{} exact-name DAVIS overlaps and
\KiRHubOverlapExcludedAdjustedRho{}
after additionally adjusting that overlap-excluded comparison for receptor-domain sequence
identity. The locked 15-pair endpoint was also recovered by centred KiRHub geometry with
AUROC \KiRHubLockedAUROC{} and average precision \KiRHubLockedAP{} (target-label QAP
$p<0.001$ for each). These centred levels support cross-panel stability of the fixed-panel
endpoint. Paired target-label relabelling also resolved the centred-minus-raw KiRHub gain
(AUROC $p=\KiRHubGainAUCLabelP{}$; AP $p=\KiRHubGainAPLabelP{}$; Holm-adjusted
$p=\KiRHubGainAUCHolmP{}$ and $p=\KiRHubGainAPHolmP{}$), but that gain did not exceed its
transformation-matched mechanical null ($p=\KiRHubGainAUCMatchedP{}$ and
$p=\KiRHubGainAPMatchedP{}$). We therefore claim the centred KiRHub level, not an informative
gain from centring. The absolute centred KiRHub AUROC and average precision each exceeded
their transformation-matched null ($p=\KiRHubAbsoluteMatchedP{}$ for both).

Centering itself, however, mechanically changes correlations. A global network QAP that
destroyed target correspondence across panels supported both absolute centred agreement
and its increment over raw agreement ($p<0.001$ and $p=\CrossPanelGlobalGainP{}$). A
complementary
transformation-matched null independently permuted ligand identities within every target
column and then repeated both transformations; it produced a mean centred-minus-raw change
larger than the observed one ($p=\CrossPanelMatchedNullP{}$). Its mean raw agreement was
$\CrossPanelMatchedRawNullMean{}$, not the observed $\CrossPanelRaw{}$, so this null evaluates
an unconditional mechanical centering gain rather than conditioning on the already aligned
raw maps. Absolute centred agreement still exceeded it
($p=\CrossPanelMatchedAbsoluteCenteredP{}$). The matched raw baselines were near chance,
rather than the already high observed raw metrics, so both gain tests are unconditional.

The single number that settles how to read this is the ratio of the observed increment to
the mechanical one. Centring raised mean cross-panel agreement by
$+\CrossPanelObservedGain{}$, from \CrossPanelRaw{} to \CrossPanelCentered{}; the
transformation-matched null, which applies the identical pair of transformations to
ligand-permuted columns, produced a mean increment of $+\CrossPanelMechanicalGain{}$. The
observed gain is thus only \CrossPanelGainOverMechanical{} times what the transformation
generates on data with no shared target structure at all, and the level---not the
increment---is what carries information here. We therefore claim only the level: the four
centred experimental geometries agree strongly and that agreement exceeds its matched null.
We do not claim that centring reveals a more faithful experimental geometry. A concrete
alternative explanation is available and untested: the panels screen different compound
libraries, and centring removes compound promiscuity, which differs between libraries, so the
rise in agreement may reflect library heterogeneity rather than biology.

\subsection{Docking--experiment agreement is path dependent}

For each assay panel, we evaluated all four cells of a $2\times2$ design: uncentred or
within-ligand-centred docking geometry crossed with uncentred or centred experimental
geometry (Table~\ref{tab:factorial}; Fig.~\ref{fig:external}a). Raw docking against raw
experiment ranged from $-0.215$ to 0.050. These negative values are not a score-direction
error: multiplying every activity or every docking column by $-1$ leaves all within-matrix
target correlations unchanged. They instead show that the ordering of target-pair
correlations differs when ligand main effects are retained.

Against the fixed centred experimental endpoint, raw docking geometry was already
positively concordant in all four panels (0.173--0.276). Within-ligand centering of docking
raised these values to 0.174--0.322. The conditional increments were 0.016 for DAVIS,
0.044 for PKIS2, 0.001 for PKIS1 and 0.045 for KiRHub; none was resolved by a paired
target-label QAP ($p=0.335$, 0.146, 0.462 and 0.126; Fig.~\ref{fig:external}a). All four
increments also failed to exceed the transformation-matched mechanical null
($p=\DavisDockingMatchedP{}$, \PkisTwoDockingMatchedP{},
\PkisOneDockingMatchedP{} and \KiRHubDockingMatchedP{}). All four
full-support increments were positive, so the result does not establish a zero docking-side effect. The
matched null began at near-zero docking--experiment concordance rather than the observed
0.173--0.276; it therefore tests an unconditional mechanical centering gain, not a gain
conditioned on the already aligned raw maps.
The panels share the same 190 target pairs and their probabilities were not pooled. The 95\%
target-label null intervals all spanned zero, with an overall envelope from $-0.086$ to
0.104; these are null intervals rather than confidence intervals for a target population.

Against the fixed uncentred experimental endpoint, the same docking transformation gave
larger increments: 0.168 for DAVIS, 0.249 for PKIS2, 0.218 for PKIS1 and 0.264 for KiRHub.
All four were resolved by the corresponding paired target-label QAP ($p=0.01512$, 0.00152,
0.00536 and 0.00008) and exceeded the transformation-matched null on its fixed 15,000-ligand
support ($p=\DavisRawEndpointDockingMatchedP{},\,
\PkisTwoRawEndpointDockingMatchedP{},\, \PkisOneRawEndpointDockingMatchedP{},\,
\text{and}\ \KiRHubRawEndpointDockingMatchedP{}$). All four target-label probabilities
remained below 0.05 after Holm correction across these four raw-endpoint tests. Thus the
apparent magnitude and statistical
resolution of the docking transformation depend on which experimental geometry is held
fixed.

\subsection{Predictive feature structure does not validate a component correlation map}

Seven ordinary ligand descriptors predicted nontrivial score structure out of chemical
group. Across repeated group partitions, their removal reduced mean squared residual target
correlation by \DffDescriptorDesignLow{}--\DffDescriptorDesignHigh{} in Docking-44 and
\DsDescriptorDesignLow{}--\DsDescriptorDesignHigh{} in DOCKSTRING-58; across five
score-blind DOCKSTRING supports the support means ranged from 0.528 to 0.545. These are
operational before/after ratios, not additive fractions of correlation explained. Each
target has its own fitted coefficient vector, so the result establishes a low-dimensional
target-indexed ligand-feature response rather than a receptor-independent physicochemical
mechanism.

Capacity-matched controls on the common-20 support after excluding structural overlaps with
DAVIS, PKIS2 and PKIS1 made that boundary sharper. The
seven physicochemical descriptors had mean out-of-fold target
$R^2=\FixedPhyschemOOFRtwo{}$ and reduced mean squared residual target correlation by
\FixedPhyschemCorrelationReduction{}. A locked seven-dimensional random projection of
count-Morgan features gave $R^2=\FixedMorganOOFRtwo{}$ and reduction
\FixedMorganCorrelationReduction{}; reductions across 20 fixed projection seeds ranged
from \FixedMorganReductionLow{} to \FixedMorganReductionHigh{}, and
\FixedMorganSeedsExceedPhyschem{}/20 exceeded the physicochemical value.
Thus the predictive signal is not specific to the named descriptors. It can be represented
by a generic low-dimensional chemical-feature basis that retains molecular size and local
topology.

A more important negative control exposed a failure mode in validating fitted components by
their target-correlation maps. Let $H$ be a shared $q$-dimensional ligand basis and $Y$ the
residual ligand-by-target score surface. Target-wise least squares in one training fold gives

\begin{equation}
\widehat B=(H_{\mathrm{tr}}^{\mathsf T}H_{\mathrm{tr}})^{-1}
H_{\mathrm{tr}}^{\mathsf T}Y_{\mathrm{tr}},\qquad
\widehat Y_{\mathrm{te}}=H_{\mathrm{te}}\widehat B.
\label{eq:nuisanceprojection}
\end{equation}

Even when $H$ is independent of ligand chemistry and scores, the target-specific columns of
$\widehat B$ form a low-rank random sketch of the training score covariance. Out-of-fold
predictive performance then tends towards zero, but column correlation divides away the
predicted columns' scale. In our stable seven-dimensional ligand-identity hash control, mean out-of-fold target
$R^2$ was \FixedHashOOFRtwo{} and mean-squared-correlation reduction was
\FixedHashCorrelationReduction{}, yet the fitted
component map correlated \FixedHashGeometryLow{}--\FixedHashGeometryHigh{} with the four
experimental maps, matching or exceeding the physicochemical component in three of four
panels (physicochemical range \FixedPhyschemGeometryLow{}--\FixedPhyschemGeometryHigh{}).
Across 20 row permutations of
the same seven-dimensional physicochemical matrix, mean target $R^2$ remained
\NuisanceOOFRtwoLow{}--\NuisanceOOFRtwoHigh{} and correlation reduction remained
\NuisanceCorrelationReductionLow{}--\NuisanceCorrelationReductionHigh{}, while component-
map agreement ranged from \NuisanceGeometryLow{} to \NuisanceGeometryHigh{} across panels
and seeds (Fig.~\ref{fig:features}; Supplementary Information). These are fixed-seed
algorithmic ranges, not confidence intervals.

The feature fits therefore support two distinct conclusions. Physicochemical and count-
Morgan bases genuinely predict part of the docking score surface out of group, whereas the
external concordance of a fitted component's correlation map is not, by itself, evidence
that the component is predictive, chemical or biological. Such a map must be accompanied by
an out-of-fold predictive-performance metric and a nuisance-basis control. We consequently do not assign
the full docking--experiment concordance to the descriptor component, its correlated
remainder, or a mediated fraction.

\subsection{Structural and measurement controls bound the target-pair map}

DAVIS required a separate measurement-limit check. Excluding the four targets with more
than 90\% of values at the reporting floor (AKT1, AKT2, MAPK1 and MAPKAPK2) left 16 targets.
On that support centred docking geometry correlated 0.210 with continuous DAVIS geometry
($p=0.030$ under 49,999 target-label permutations) and 0.197 with binary above-floor status
geometry ($p=0.037$). The two experimental geometries correlated 0.811
(Fig.~\ref{fig:external}d), indicating that the continuous DAVIS map largely tracked whether
measurements lay above the reporting floor on this support. This sensitivity
supports a coarse co-response or reporting-status pattern on the fixed panel; it does not
turn floor-censored DAVIS values into finely resolved quantitative affinities.

Sequence and pocket similarity produced comparable or higher point estimates than Vina
geometry for the experimental target-pair pattern (Fig.~\ref{fig:external}b). A target pair was labelled
positive when it lay in the centred upper decile in at least two of DAVIS, PKIS2 and PKIS1;
this yielded 15 of 190 pairs and was fixed before KiRHub was inspected. Receptor-domain
sequence identity retrieved those pairs with AUROC 0.718 and average precision 0.426;
KLIFS 85-residue pocket identity gave 0.708 and 0.486. Raw Vina geometry gave 0.597 and
0.236, while centred Vina gave 0.710 and 0.301. Because the positives were defined from
centred experimental geometries, this raw-versus-centred retrieval gap is conditional on the
chosen endpoint and cannot be interpreted as a causal or transformation-neutral benefit of
centring. In the declared 5--25\% threshold-family omnibus, the mean centred-minus-raw
gain was unresolved (target-label QAP $p=0.061$ for AUROC and $p=0.076$ for average
precision), limiting the result to an upper-tail finding rather than a threshold-general
improvement. With only 15 positives, these values are bounding diagnostics rather than
evidence that one predictor outperforms another.
Binary indicators of shared KLIFS group or family also retrieved this endpoint above their
unrestricted label nulls (Supplementary Information), reinforcing the need to
condition biological interpretations on kinase taxonomy.

Continuous adjustment gave the same boundary. After rank-linear adjustment for both
receptor-domain sequence identity and KLIFS pocket identity, centred Vina geometry retained
a partial Spearman correlation of \ThreePanelPartialRho{} with the mean of DAVIS, PKIS2
and PKIS1 (unrestricted target-label QAP $p=\ThreePanelUnrestrictedP{}$) and
\KiRHubPartialRho{} with KiRHub ($p=\KiRHubUnrestrictedP{}$). When target labels could be
exchanged only within KLIFS kinase groups, the corresponding probabilities were
\ThreePanelRestrictedP{} and \KiRHubRestrictedP{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:external}c). The
association therefore remains positive after the tested continuous similarities under an
unrestricted null, but it is unresolved within coarse kinase groups. Group-level kinase
organization remains a plausible explanation; the analysis does not establish independent
within-family discrimination. The effective randomization units are targets, not 190
independent dyads: the restricted null moves 17 labels in blocks of 11, 3 and 3 and leaves
three singleton targets fixed. Its empirical one-sided 5\% critical correlations after
sequence and pocket adjustment were 0.360 for the three-panel mean and 0.376 for KiRHub;
the corresponding null medians were 0.103 and 0.161. Crossing the critical boundary would
require increases of 0.136 and 0.096 over the observed values.
These are fixed-matrix randomization-resolution diagnostics, not power calculations or
population confidence limits.

A 12-target mixed-family sensitivity was 91.6\% right-censored. Family-preserving QAP did
not resolve centred-minus-raw gains for the released-bound, 10-$\mu$M or 30-$\mu$M
endpoints (one-sided $p=0.729$, 0.939 and 0.714, respectively). The corresponding point
estimates and unrestricted tests are reported in Supplementary Information.

\subsection{Target-pair geometry is not a ligand-wise target-ranking rule}

Within-ligand centering has an exact operational limitation. If
$R_{ij}=X_{ij}-\bar X_{i\cdot}$, then for any two targets $j$ and $k$,
\begin{equation}
R_{ij}-R_{ik}=X_{ij}-X_{ik}.
\label{eq:rankinvariance}
\end{equation}
Subtracting one ligand-specific constant cannot change that ligand's target order. Relative
to absolute scores, target-specific offsets and scales can change order; the row subtraction
alone cannot. Accordingly, target-centred and two-way-centred unscaled scores had exactly
the same broad-benchmark observed-pair concordance of 0.533.

The primary operational test used the broad DOCKSTRING--ChEMBL overlap. It retained
\BroadRankingRetainedLigands{} ligands with at least two exact-relation experimental cells;
\BroadRankingEvaluatedLigands{} contributed at least one non-tied comparison and
\BroadRankingUninformativeLigands{} did not. The evaluated support contained
\BroadRankingTargets{} of the 58 DOCKSTRING targets, \BroadRankingCells{} observed cells and
\BroadRankingPairs{} within-ligand target-pair comparisons. It measures concordance only on
targets that ChEMBL happened to report for each ligand, not retrieval of unmeasured targets.

Absolute Vina, column-standardized scores and scaled two-way residuals gave mean
observed-pair concordances \BroadRankingAbsolute{}, \BroadRankingColumn{} and
\BroadRankingResidual{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:ranking}a). Thus absolute Vina had the highest
aggregate point estimate among the tested docking-derived representations, but not among all
references. A target-only
prior fitted after holding out the evaluated ligand's complete scaffold cluster reached
\BroadRankingCohortPrior{}, compared with \BroadRankingDockingPrior{} for a prior fitted on
the external docking library and \BroadRankingExternalPrior{} for a cohort-excluded ChEMBL
prior. The scaffold-held-out cohort prior uses outcomes from other benchmark ligands and is
a diagnostic of target prevalence and assay-selection structure, not a deployable
ligand-specific predictor. Absolute Vina exceeded the ligand-invariant target-only prior
fitted on the external DOCKSTRING reference by only
\BroadRankingAbsoluteMinusDockingPrior{} in paired comparisons, with a
conservative chemical-cluster 95\% interval from
\BroadRankingAbsoluteMinusDockingPriorLow{} to
\BroadRankingAbsoluteMinusDockingPriorHigh{}. The broad benchmark therefore did not resolve
ligand-specific ordering information beyond this target-only docking baseline.

The paired residual-minus-absolute difference was \BroadRankingDelta{}. Chemical-cluster
resampling, conditional on the 31 fixed target labels, gave a 95\% interval of
\BroadRankingSamplingLow{} to \BroadRankingSamplingHigh{}. Delete-one-target jackknifing
instead probed panel composition and gave the much wider approximation
\BroadRankingTargetJackknifeLow{} to \BroadRankingTargetJackknifeHigh{}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ranking}b). These are deliberately separate uncertainty layers: neither a
target-label QAP nor the transformation-matched geometry null is a sampling interval for
ligand-wise concordance. The corresponding residual-minus-column-standardized difference
was near zero and unresolved under both layers (Fig.~\ref{fig:ranking}b,c).

The less assay-heterogeneous, human-binding Ki/Kd-restricted arm retained
\BroadHumanKiKdRetainedLigands{} ligands,
of which \BroadHumanKiKdEvaluatedLigands{} contributed \BroadHumanKiKdPairs{} non-tied
pairs over \BroadHumanKiKdTargets{} targets. Absolute and scaled-residual concordance were
\BroadHumanKiKdAbsolute{} and \BroadHumanKiKdResidual{}, a paired difference of
\BroadHumanKiKdDelta{} (chemical-cluster 95\% interval
\BroadHumanKiKdSamplingLow{} to \BroadHumanKiKdSamplingHigh{}; target-jackknife
approximation \BroadHumanKiKdTargetJackknifeLow{} to
\BroadHumanKiKdTargetJackknifeHigh{}; Fig.~\ref{fig:ranking}b). This arm can still compare a
Ki-derived cell with a Kd-derived cell within one ligand and is therefore endpoint-restricted,
not homogeneous. Of \BroadHumanKiKdPairs{} pairs,
\BroadHumanKiKdSameEndpointPairs{} (\BroadHumanKiKdSameEndpointPercent{}) compared the same
single endpoint, \BroadHumanKiKdMixedEndpointPairs{}
(\BroadHumanKiKdMixedEndpointPercent{}) were unambiguous Ki--Kd comparisons and
\BroadHumanKiKdPooledCellPairs{} (\BroadHumanKiKdPooledCellPercent{}) involved at least one
cell pooled across both endpoint types. The point estimate favoured absolute Vina, but neither
uncertainty layer resolved the contrast. Separate endpoint arms were heterogeneous: Ki was
negative with a chemical-cluster interval excluding zero
($\BroadSingleKiDelta{}$, $\BroadSingleKiLow{}$ to $\BroadSingleKiHigh{}$), whereas the much
smaller Kd arm reversed sign ($\BroadSingleKdDelta{}$, $\BroadSingleKdLow{}$ to
$\BroadSingleKdHigh{}$); IC50 and EC50 were unresolved. These post hoc arms expose
endpoint dependence and are not four confirmatory tests.

The old Docking-44--ChEMBL analysis is retained only as a sparse support sensitivity. It
evaluated \SparseRankingEvaluatedLigands{} ligands, \SparseRankingTargets{} targets and
\SparseRankingPairs{} non-tied pairs; its residual-minus-absolute interval was
\RankingResidualAbsoluteLow{} to \RankingResidualAbsoluteHigh{}. The two docking matrices
share ChEMBL as an activity source, so they are alternative docking supports for the same
observed-pair estimand rather than independent experimental replications.

Two denser full-Standard-InChIKey-matched checks narrowed chemical-sampling uncertainty but
not target-composition uncertainty. DAVIS contained \DenseDavisEvaluatedLigands{} evaluated
ligands and \DenseDavisPairs{} orderable comparisons across \DenseDavisTargets{} targets;
PKIS2 contained \DensePkisTwoEvaluatedLigands{} ligands and \DensePkisTwoPairs{} comparisons
across \DensePkisTwoTargets{} targets. Residual-minus-absolute differences were
\DenseDavisDelta{} (chemical-cluster 95\% interval \DenseDavisDeltaLow{} to
\DenseDavisDeltaHigh{}) and \DensePkisTwoDelta{} (\DensePkisTwoDeltaLow{} to

\DenseDavisTargetJackknifeLow{} to \DenseDavisTargetJackknifeHigh{} and

comparisons gave \DensePkisTwoBothActiveDelta{}
(\DensePkisTwoBothActiveDeltaLow{} to \DensePkisTwoBothActiveDeltaHigh{}). Because the
experimental outcomes define stratum membership and the intervals are unadjusted for
multiplicity, these positive strata are hypothesis-generating and do not replace the
aggregate estimands.

The stepwise decomposition identifies exactly where rankings can change. Let $\mu_j$ and
$s_j$ be a reference target mean and residual standard deviation,
$\bar X_{i\cdot}$ the evaluation-ligand row mean and $\bar X_{\mathrm{ref}}$ the reference
grand mean. A target-centred scaled score $S_{ij}$ and scaled two-way residual $T_{ij}$
satisfy
\begin{equation}
T_{ij}-S_{ij}
=-\frac{\bar X_{i\cdot}-\bar X_{\mathrm{ref}}}{s_j}.
\label{eq:scalinginteraction}
\end{equation}
Across the evaluation matrix this difference is the outer product of a ligand-specific
scalar and the fixed inverse-scale target profile, hence a rank-one correction. It is largest
for targets with small residual spread and changes sign with the ligand's row mean. Thus row
subtraction alone is rank invariant (Eq.~\ref{eq:rankinvariance}); unequal target scaling is
the operation that turns it into a different score rule. The correlated step contrasts are
post hoc and multiplicity-unadjusted and are reported in Supplementary Information as a
localization of the rule difference, not as separate discoveries.

Across the broad primary and two dense checks, no tested normalized Vina representation had
a higher aggregate point estimate than absolute Vina. This is a result on the measured pairs
and fixed target panels, not evidence of equivalence, absence of subgroup effects or failure
to retrieve targets that were never assayed.

Coverage, binding-assay, same-endpoint, activity-threshold, pair-weighted and chemical-
de-redundancy analyses are reported in Supplementary Information. Their ordering is not
uniform, reinforcing the restricted interpretation: the broad result is an observed-pair
stress test under a sparse, non-random measurement process, not a reverse-docking retrieval
benchmark or a general statement about selectivity.

\section{Discussion}

The main practical result is that a docking target-correlation map is conditional on the
observed source library used to estimate it, but inexpensive to remeasure within that library.
In exploratory post hoc analyses, maps from the lowest and highest molecular-weight domains agreed at only 0.205 and 0.351 in
the two Vina panels, whereas chemically disjoint controls with matched molecular-weight
distributions agreed at 0.991 and 0.994. This gap is too large to attribute to finite sample
size or chemical disjointness alone: the shift was also present on the raw surfaces, whereas
MW-stratified chemical-group-disjoint halves made separately within each extreme band
remained highly concordant. The largest shifts across the seven inspected descriptor
splits formed a correlated molecular-size family, while other descriptor splits were weaker;
this does not identify a unique causal descriptor. Conversely, 200 same-distribution ligands recovered the
full-library maps at 0.942 and 0.918, with nearly unchanged agreement when the sampled
chemical groups were excluded from the reference. A practitioner therefore need not dock an
entire screening collection to diagnose panel redundancy in these two data sets: a representative probe set of a
few hundred compounds gave high descriptive agreement on the observed supports. This does
not establish 200 ligands as a universal accuracy threshold. The estimate should be repeated after a
material chemical-domain shift and reported with the scoring pipeline, receptor panel and
ligand support; it should not be exported as an intrinsic network of the targets.

The spectral analysis explains why this distinction matters. Both matrices contain a nearly
uniform ligand-wide score axis. Within-ligand centring removes that axis and exposes a
higher-dimensional, still correlated residual map; PR merely places this dependence on an
effective-component scale equivalent to mean squared target correlation. Physicochemical
and count-Morgan bases both predicted part of the score surface out of chemical group, so
the low-dimensional response was not specific to seven named descriptors. More strikingly,
a stable-hash nuisance basis had essentially zero held-out predictive performance while its
fitted-component correlation map still resembled the experimental maps. Separate target
coefficients inherit a random sketch of target covariance, and correlation removes the
predicted columns' scale. Component-map concordance must therefore be paired with out-of-fold
predictive-performance and nuisance controls. Descriptor removal also restored only part of low/high-
domain agreement: molecular size is a domain marker, not an identified sole cause, and
neither residual PR nor a fitted-component map is a docking-quality score.

External agreement supplies a further boundary rather than a biological certificate. A
matched raw-to-centred comparison changes predictor and endpoint together. Holding the
experimental endpoint fixed, the docking-side increments were small and unresolved, whereas
the same docking transformation produced much larger increments against the uncentred
endpoint. Sequence and KLIFS pocket similarity had comparable or higher retrieval point
estimates for the fixed co-selective pairs than centred Vina, and strict within-group
permutations did not resolve an
additional within-family component. Because even a nonpredictive nuisance component could
inherit this external geometry, decomposing the surface into a fitted component and its
correlated remainder cannot localize the agreement. A docking map's concordance with an
assay panel therefore describes co-response on the tested support; by itself it does not
establish target-resolved recognition.

For ranking, the boundary is quantitative rather than merely cautionary. Subtracting a
ligand row mean cannot reorder that ligand's targets, so no diagnostic built on the residual
map is itself a ranking result. Unequal target scaling turns that otherwise invariant row
term into an exact rank-one correction and therefore defines a new scoring rule. In the broad
primary benchmark absolute Vina had the highest aggregate point estimate among the tested
docking-derived representations, but a scaffold-
held-out target-prevalence prior was higher than every ligand-specific rule. Chemical-
cluster inference also did not resolve absolute Vina's small advantage over the external-
docking target-only prior. Thus this sparse observed-pair benchmark did not establish that
the ligand-specific Vina scores added ordering information beyond target prevalence.
Chemical-
sampling intervals for residual-minus-absolute scores were narrow, whereas target-jackknife
sensitivities were much wider. The less heterogeneous, endpoint-restricted human binding
Ki/Kd arm also favoured absolute Vina in point estimate, but both uncertainty layers included
zero. That arm can still mix Ki- and Kd-derived cells within a pair, and the separate Ki and
Kd arms differed in sign.
This endpoint dependence precludes a universal winner. Normalization-path contrasts and the
margin curves retained in Supplementary Information are post hoc localizations, not
confirmatory evidence.

This task dependence is consistent with earlier benchmarking. MASC did not generally
improve enrichment across eight targets and ten scoring functions; multiple-target
screening showed that MASC-like decisions depend on the reference receptor set; and
heavy-atom normalization improved most tested GOLD panels while impairing most Glide
panels \cite{jacobsson2006,fukunishi2006mts,ibrahim2015}. Normalization is therefore a
task-specific scoring rule, not a universally beneficial correction. The separation the
audit enforces---between a co-response geometry over targets and a decision about one
compound---is the same separation that CASF draws between the scoring, ranking and screening
powers of a function \cite{su2019casf}, and the target-pair estimand is the descriptive
counterpart of the ligand--target interaction terms that proteochemometric models fit
explicitly \cite{cortesciriano2015,vanwesten2011}.

Both large matrices use Vina-family scores. In a secondary fixed-pose sensitivity, five of
six non-Vina functions met weak shared-axis criteria on Vina-selected poses, while PLECscore
falsified a universal-threshold claim. Pose selection remained Vina dependent and several
functions share training data. Moreover, a broadly positive score matrix can yield a nearly
uniform leading vector under these weak criteria. This is neither an independent replication
of a complete docking pipeline nor evidence of scorer-generality.

The remaining limits are as follows. The experimental
comparison is restricted to 20 kinases and observed target pairs; the target panels are not
random samples from protein space. DAVIS, PKIS2, PKIS1 and KiRHub differ in assay technology,
compound libraries and measurement scale. DAVIS has \DavisFloorPercent{} of fixed-panel values at its
pKd floor, and its continuous target map largely tracks binary above-floor status
($\rho=\DavisStatusGeometryRho{}$ on the fixed 20-target panel). Its sensitivity therefore
supports coarse co-response or measurement-limit status more strongly than fine quantitative
affinity geometry; KiRHub has \KiRHubCeilingPercent{} exact residual-activity
values at 100 and lacks structures needed for a complete chemical-overlap audit. The
structural controls are post hoc, and exchangeability within
KLIFS groups is itself an approximation. The small-panel recovery experiment uses
same-distribution samples and its high agreement is descriptive for the two observed source
libraries, not a universal 200-ligand accuracy threshold. The broad ligand-wise ChEMBL
benchmark is non-randomly observed and assay heterogeneous; its smaller human-binding Ki/Kd
arm is less heterogeneous but can still compare Ki- and Kd-derived cells.
The dense DAVIS block is strongly floor-censored, PKIS2 is a single-dose inhibition panel,
and exact matching to DOCKSTRING selects restricted chemical subsets. Chemical intervals
are conditional on 21 fixed targets in the dense checks and 31 fixed targets in the broad
ChEMBL analysis; the original sparse Docking-44 sensitivity uses 38 targets. Target
jackknives are panel-composition sensitivities rather than population intervals. Finally,
neither PR nor external target-pair concordance assesses docking poses or
absolute affinity accuracy.

The reusable audit follows directly from these boundaries: state the score and endpoint
estimands; report both uncentred and within-ligand-centred correlation geometry; express PR
beside mean squared correlation and the full spectrum; use transformation-matched nulls;
quantify chemical and target-panel sensitivity; validate target-pair geometry and
ligand-wise ranking separately; pair any fitted-component map with held-out predictive performance and a
nuisance basis; and report the full predictor-by-endpoint table whenever both sides are
normalized. The released generic command-line tool implements the spectral
portion; dataset-specific scripts implement the external-geometry and ranking steps.
Table~\ref{tab:auditprotocol} makes the associated decisions explicit.

\section{Conclusions}

A Vina target-correlation map is a property of a scoring pipeline, receptor panel and ligand
library together. In exploratory post hoc analyses within the observed source libraries, it
changed sharply between molecular-weight-defined domains, with the strongest inspected shifts
belonging to a correlated molecular-size descriptor family. Descriptive sampling experiments
gave high same-domain recovery from 200--500 representative ligands in these two data sets;
that range is not a universal accuracy threshold. The actionable protocol is therefore to
estimate the map on a small probe set drawn from the intended
library, use it to diagnose redundant target responses, and re-estimate it after domain
shift. Within-ligand centring exposes the residual map but does not validate it: a
low-dimensional ligand-feature basis predicts part of its score structure, yet even a
nonpredictive nuisance basis can show nonzero component-map agreement after correlation
removes the predicted columns' scale. Component-map agreement alone is therefore
non-identifying; assay transformation and protein-family structure impose additional
interpretive limits.

The map is also not a compound-level target-ranking rule. Subtracting a ligand's row mean
cannot change its target order; unequal target scales turn that invariant subtraction into
an exact rank-one correction that must be assessed as a new score rule. On the broad
observed-pair benchmark, absolute Vina had the highest aggregate point estimate among the
tested docking-derived representations, while chemical-sampling and target-composition
analyses did not resolve a gain for scaled
residual scores; nor did the paired chemical-cluster interval resolve its small advantage
over a target-only prior fitted on the external DOCKSTRING reference. Spectral diagnosis,
biological validation and ligand-wise ranking are thus
three separate tasks: the first can be performed cheaply, but neither of the latter two
follows from an increase in residual effective dimension.
