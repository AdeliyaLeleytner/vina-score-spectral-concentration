\sisubsection{S4.1 Quartile contrast and matched chemical-group controls}

Molecular weight was chosen after preliminary inspection and is treated as a post-hoc domain
coordinate. It was computed with RDKit 2025.03.6 on the frozen analysis SMILES
\cite{rdkit2025}. The Docking-44 contrast uses all 12{,}651 rows: the low band is
$\mathrm{MW}\leq 218.300$ and the high band $\mathrm{MW}\geq 349.333$, 3{,}163 rows each, with
band median molecular weights 178.054 and 413.602~g/mol. The DOCKSTRING-58 contrast uses the
score-blind seed-71 support of 15{,}000 complete rows: the low band is
$\mathrm{MW}\leq 325.368$ and the high band $\mathrm{MW}\geq 449.491$, 3{,}751 and 3{,}750
rows, with band medians 286.310 and 498.573~g/mol. Each band gets its own raw and
row-centred residual target map, and the two bands are compared by Spearman correlation of
corresponding strict-upper-triangle entries.

The matched control is 200 splits of the same library into halves that share no whole chemical
group, drawn with seed 20260812. Chemical groups are the 8{,}150 frozen
\texttt{Butina\_clusters} labels for Docking-44, treated as opaque by the producer, and 11{,}905
Bemis--Murcko scaffold groups on the DOCKSTRING support, where every acyclic molecule is a
source-row singleton \cite{butina1999,murcko1996}. The global matched control assigns whole
groups to two halves inside ten stable rank strata of group-median molecular weight, greedily
balancing ligand counts; group overlap between halves is 0 by construction and the mean
absolute difference in median molecular weight between halves is 0.80~g/mol for Docking-44 and
1.32~g/mol for DOCKSTRING. The within-band control repeats the same whole-group split after
restricting to one molecular-weight band.

In all four rows of Table~\ref{tab:s4controls} the observed value falls below the 2.5th
percentile of the global matched control and below the 2.5th percentile of both within-band
controls. The gap between the observed contrast and the controls is therefore not explained by
finite-sample noise, and not by generic chemical disjointness at matched size.

Sign flips separate the two maps more sharply than the rank correlations do. Between the two
residual quartile maps, 42.5\% of target-pair correlations change sign on Docking-44 and
36.7\% on DOCKSTRING-58. In the matched control the median residual sign-flip fraction is
3.8\% [2.7\%, 5.4\%] on Docking-44 and 2.8\% [2.3\%, 3.5\%] on DOCKSTRING-58. Between the two
raw quartile maps only 2.7\% and 1.8\% of pairs change sign, against control medians of 0.2\%
and 0.4\%. A shared positive component holds the raw signs in place inside each band: the raw
participation ratio is 1.187 in the low Docking-44 band and 2.995 in the high band, against
13.625 and 10.147 for the residual maps of the same two bands. On DOCKSTRING-58 the raw values
are 1.751 and 3.080 against 19.552 and 23.800.

These comparisons diagnose dependence on the chemical domain a map was estimated on. Molecular
weight is correlated with other ligand properties and was selected post hoc, so nothing here
identifies it as the causal coordinate.

\sisubsection{S4.2 Threshold, continuum and support-seed sensitivities}

The quartile boundary at 0.25 per tail is one choice out of many. A separate exact-rank sweep
used tail fractions 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30, 0.35 and 0.40. Tails are exact equal-$N$ rank
tails; rows are sorted by molecular weight and then by source-row index, so boundary ties
resolve deterministically. Each threshold carries 100 equal-$N$ random-disjoint controls.

On the residual map, agreement between the low and high tails runs from 0.201 to 0.259 across
the 7 thresholds on Docking-44, median 0.210, and from 0.174 to 0.528 on DOCKSTRING-58, median
0.351. On the raw map the ranges are 0.175 to 0.351, median 0.227, and 0.134 to 0.598, median
0.409. The matched random-disjoint control means run from 0.978 to 0.995 on Docking-44 residual
maps and from 0.976 to 0.994 on DOCKSTRING-58 residual maps. At every one of the 7 thresholds,
in all four dataset-by-map combinations, the observed value falls below the 2.5th percentile of
its matched control. Per-threshold rows are in
\path{results/revision_map_decision_sensitivities/mw_threshold_sweep.csv} and the compact form
in \path{.../mw_threshold_sweep_summary.csv}.

Tails discard the middle of the library, so we also cut the same libraries into ten
non-overlapping equal-population molecular-weight bins and compared all 45 bin pairs. Any
$N \bmod 10$ highest-rank remainder rows are excluded and counted in
\path{.../mw_decile_maps.csv}. Across the 45 pairs, the Spearman correlation between separation
of bin median molecular weights and residual-map dissimilarity is 0.945 on Docking-44 and 0.988
on DOCKSTRING-58; on raw maps it is 0.814 and 0.925. A descriptive ordinary-least-squares slope
puts the residual-map dissimilarity at 0.220 per 100~Da on Docking-44 and 0.316 per 100~Da on
DOCKSTRING-58. Pairwise residual agreement ranges from 0.153 to 0.933 on Docking-44 and from
0.174 to 0.962 on DOCKSTRING-58. The 45 comparisons reuse ten maps and are not independent
observations, so no significance calculation is attached to the trend.

The DOCKSTRING quartile contrast depends on which 15{,}000 rows were drawn, so it was repeated
on six independently seeded score-blind supports of that size, seeds 71, 72, 73, 74, 75 and
20260809. Residual-map agreement runs from 0.325 to 0.361, median 0.346; raw-map agreement runs
from 0.377 to 0.409, median 0.398. The residual sign-flip fraction runs from 35.9\% to 37.5\%
and the raw sign-flip fraction from 1.7\% to 2.4\%. Each support carries a SHA-256 fingerprint
of its selected complete-row index in \path{summary.json}, and per-seed rows are in
\path{results/public_chemical_domain_controls/dockstring_support_seed_contrasts.csv}. This is a
range across six named seeds, not an uncertainty interval.

\sisubsection{S4.3 Descriptor-axis specificity and the non-predictive-basis control}

To see whether the molecular-weight result generalises to any coarse chemical coordinate, the
same inclusive lower/upper 25\% threshold family was applied post hoc to seven RDKit
descriptors. Three axes are designated size-related: heavy-atom count, molecular weight and
Labute ASA. The other four are TPSA, cLogP, rotatable-bond count and ring count. Inclusive
empirical quartile thresholds retain boundary ties, so discrete descriptors give unequal tails
that can run far past a quarter of the library: the two disjoint DOCKSTRING ring-count tails
hold 8{,}376 and 6{,}624 rows, jointly all 15{,}000. Docking-44 again uses all 12{,}651 rows
and DOCKSTRING the fixed seed-71 support.

Across the three size-related axes, residual-map agreement spans 0.200 to 0.207, median 0.205,
on Docking-44, and 0.322 to 0.361, median 0.351, on DOCKSTRING-58. Across the four other coarse
axes the spans are 0.703 to 0.887, median 0.771, and 0.787 to 0.831, median 0.812. Every one of
the 14 observed contrasts falls below the 2.5th percentile of its matched control, so the
result is graded rather than a size-versus-nothing dichotomy: the largest changes follow size,
and the other four axes also move the map more than finite support alone would.

The three size-related axes are one family, not three replications. Heavy-atom count and Labute
ASA correlate at Spearman 0.985 on Docking-44 and 0.992 on DOCKSTRING-58; heavy-atom count and
molecular weight at 0.935 and 0.965; molecular weight and Labute ASA at 0.968 and 0.979
(\path{.../descriptor_correlations.csv}). Control replicates are also shared: contrasts with an
identical pair of tail sizes reuse the same cached series of 200 random splits, so the 14 rows
of Table~\ref{tab:s4descriptors} do not stand for 14 independent control simulations.

The second control asks a different question: does agreement between a fitted target-pair
geometry and an experimental panel require the fitted basis to predict anything? It runs on a
fixed 20-target de-leaked DOCKSTRING support of 259{,}579 ligands and 5{,}191{,}580 cells, after
removing 481 rows matching 1{,}050 assay connectivity blocks, with positive scores clipped to
zero. Each basis carries 7 columns, is fitted in 5 Bemis--Murcko-scaffold folds, is subtracted
from the score surface out of fold, and the remaining target-pair geometry over the 190 dyads is
compared with a fixed centred experimental edge vector from each panel. Six bases were run
through those identical steps: the seven published RDKit physicochemical descriptors, a frozen
7-D random projection of unnormalised Morgan count fingerprints (seed 20260910, representative
of a 20-seed ensemble), two 7-D projections of normalised Morgan bit fingerprints, a
stable-hash nuisance basis, and an outcome-derived rank-7 SVD oracle that uses held-out docking
outcomes for its projection and is not a predictor. The stable-hash basis is a keyed BLAKE2b
digest of the exact input SMILES mapped to seven centred uniform coordinates, seed 20260823. It
carries no chemistry: its maximum absolute Pearson correlation with any published descriptor is
0.0063 and its maximum absolute within-basis correlation is 0.0056.

The stable-hash basis predicts nothing. Its mean out-of-fold target $R^2$ is
$-3.6\times 10^{-5}$, with median $-3.5\times 10^{-5}$, minimum $-7.3\times 10^{-5}$ and
maximum $9.5\times 10^{-6}$; subtracting its prediction reduces the mean squared off-diagonal
target correlation by a relative $2.1\times 10^{-5}$. The physicochemical basis reaches mean
out-of-fold $R^2$ 0.107 and a relative reduction of 0.234 on the same support.

It still reproduces panel geometry agreement in the band the predictive bases occupy. Its
Spearman agreement with the fixed centred experimental edge vector is 0.248 on DAVIS, 0.250 on
PKIS2, 0.103 on PKIS1 and 0.348 on KiRHub, a 0.10 to 0.35 band. The physicochemical basis gives
0.256, 0.218, 0.097 and 0.288 on the same four panels, so the nuisance basis lands within 0.008
of it on DAVIS and above it on the other three. The observed residual surface itself gives
0.280, 0.287, 0.174 and 0.322, and the SVD oracle gives 0.268, 0.267, 0.149 and 0.319. All
values are in
\path{results/descriptor_rank_matched_controls/panel_geometry_agreement.csv} and
\path{.../predictive_metrics.csv}.

This sets a limit on how any absolute agreement number can be read. A seven-column basis with
no chemical content and no held-out predictive performance still reaches 0.10 to 0.35 agreement
with experimental target-pair geometry on these panels. Absolute agreement with an experimental
panel is therefore weak evidence on its own, and cannot by itself orient an interpretation
towards the features a basis was built from. The comparison that survives this control is the
paired one: the same docking scores, raw against centred, held against one fixed experimental
endpoint, with the target panel and the experimental edges unchanged on both sides of the
pair. That is the form every experimental contrast in the main text takes.

The boundary on this control is narrow. It tests basis specificity for one fixed common-20
de-leaked DOCKSTRING geometry and is not extrapolated to the Docking-44 or DOCKSTRING-58
supports that carry the headline numbers. Its bootstrap reweights coarsened blocks of chemical
groups at 5{,}000 repeats with seed 20260824, holding the target panel and the experimental
edges fixed, so those intervals are sensitivity ranges and not confidence intervals for a
population of targets or assays.
