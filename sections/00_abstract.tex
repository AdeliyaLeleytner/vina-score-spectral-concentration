\section*{Abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Background.}
Target--target correlations in multi-target docking matrices are often read as cross-reactivity
maps. Yet a ligand that scores favourably against one receptor often scores favourably against
many, creating a shared axis that can obscure target-specific relationships. We tested this axis
across scoring functions, matched nulls and external pharmacology.

\noindent\textbf{Results.}
Eight scoring outputs evaluated on the same fixed poses all had a near-uniform leading component.
After subtracting each ligand's mean across targets, the residual geometry was more concentrated
than four transformation-matched nulls. The map was also library-dependent: estimates from the lower
and upper molecular-weight quartiles agreed only weakly. On a Psychoactive Drug Screening Program
(PDSP) target-pair graph built from quantified affinities, the residual map agreed more closely with experiment than the raw map after
Docking-44 ligands sharing a PDSP connectivity block were excluded.
This advantage weakened when censored assay cells were restored and largely disappeared when the
docking map was rebuilt using only compounds with complete docking scores. Evidence elsewhere was mixed: centred
docking approached, but did not exceed, sequence identity on a kinase endpoint, and the
raw-to-centred change reversed on KiRHub. A few hundred randomly selected ligands closely recovered
both full residual maps.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions.}
Raw docking correlations should be checked for a shared ligand-wide score offset, and the centred
map computed and interpreted alongside the raw one rather than in place of it. The centred map
retains structured geometry that is inexpensive to estimate and informative on some diversified
panels, but it depends on the ligand library, reverses on one kinase panel, and is not a
compound-level target-ranking rule.

\noindent\textbf{Scientific Contribution.}
Ligand-wise normalisation is established; our contribution is a scorer-controlled, null-calibrated
audit of the resulting geometry against pharmacology and sequence-based baselines, together with a
cost analysis and tested map-diagnostic command-line tool.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} molecular docking; scoring functions;
target-correlation map; secondary pharmacology; target cross-reactivity;
effective dimension; reproducibility
