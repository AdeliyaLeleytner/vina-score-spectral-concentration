\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics{figures/v5/fig1_shared_axis.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{A near-uniform leading component appears in all eight scoring outputs.}
Eight scoring outputs were evaluated on the same 2{,}000-ligand by 9-target block of retained
DOCKSTRING poses. Rows are grouped by feature class. (a)~PC1 variance fraction. (b)~Cosine of the
PC1 loading vector with the uniform target direction. (c)~Participation-ratio dimension before
(open marker) and after (arrowhead) removal of each ligand's mean. Bars in (a) and (b) are the
conservative union of two cluster-bootstrap intervals. Dashed lines show the descriptive criteria
recorded for this analysis; their timing cannot be verified from the release, and PLECscore falls
outside two point-estimate gates.
Because every output uses retained Vina poses and several scorers share a lineage, this is a
fixed-pose sensitivity analysis, not a comparison of independent docking pipelines.}
\label{fig:axis}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics{figures/v5/fig2_residual_structure.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Residual structure exceeds matched-null expectations and depends on ligand support.}
(a)~Residual participation ratio, scaled by its $P-1$ upper bound, for the observed matrices and
four nulls passed through the same centring pipeline on identical 12{,}000-ligand supports. Points
and bars show null medians and 2.5--97.5\% simulation ranges over 500 repetitions. (b)~Edge-rank
agreement between residual maps from the lower and upper molecular-weight quartiles (coloured
markers), compared with 200 size-matched splits sharing no whole chemical group (grey ranges).
Molecular weight was selected after preliminary inspection and marks a domain contrast; it is not
identified as the cause of the map change.}
\label{fig:residual}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics{figures/v5/fig3_external_agreement.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Raw and centred maps differ in panel-dependent ways.}
Open markers show raw docking maps and filled markers residual maps; each arrow holds the
experimental map fixed. (a)~Spearman agreement with Psychoactive Drug Screening Program (PDSP)
and Novartis Safety Profiling Database (SPD) target-pair maps. The PDSP row uses the primary
quantified-$K_\text{i}$ graph after Docking-44 rows sharing a PDSP connectivity block were excluded.
Both assay transforms are shown for SPD after DOCKSTRING rows sharing exact identities or
connectivity blocks were excluded. (b)~Upper-decile target-pair retrieval. Identity baselines are
marked on the kinase row: residual docking approaches them on ROC-AUC but remains below them on
average precision. The KiRHub arrow reverses, providing a non-replication. PDSP values use quantified
$K_\text{i}$ measurements; restoring right-censored cells weakens their agreement, and rebuilding
the docking map on complete-case chemical support largely removes the separation.}
\label{fig:external}
\end{figure}

\input{sections/05_results_cost.tex}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics{figures/v5/fig4_cost_and_core.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{A few hundred docked ligands recover the source-library map.}
(a)~Edge-rank agreement between maps estimated from random calibration samples and the full-matrix
map. Lines are means and bands are 2.5--97.5\% ranges over 100 samples per size. At 200 ligands the
DOCKSTRING design uses about 1{,}300-fold fewer score evaluations. This is recovery of docking
geometry on the source library, not affinity prediction. (b)~Change in external agreement after
the exploratory truncation to the five leading residual modes. Point estimates of rank agreement and ROC-AUC rise on all
four panels; average precision rises on two. The five-mode rule uses docking eigenvalues only, but
the six-test sensitivity is unresolved and the external comparison remains post hoc.}
\label{fig:cost}
\end{figure}
